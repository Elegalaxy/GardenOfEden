# Garden of Eden Living Game Design

Status: Active design. Milestones M0 (scaffold), M1 (headless sim core), M2 (canvas and loop), M3 (full survival loop), M4 (player tools), M5 (observability), M6 (trait inheritance with mutation), M7a (ruin node), M7b (water node and thirst), M8 (world events), M9 (save and load), M10 (family knowledge sharing), M11 (lineage records), M12 (long-run tuning), M13 (the simulation runs in a Web Worker) and M14 (the family tree view) are built; the MVP is complete.

## How to read this document

Every rule carries a status tag. Do not treat a rule as decided unless it says `[CONFIRMED]`.

- `[CONFIRMED]` The user decided or approved this. Changing it needs a new user decision.
- `[PROVISIONAL]` A default that is built or planned but not settled. Numbers in particular are tuning hypotheses, not balance claims. They live in `src/content/tuning.ts` and `src/content/nodeDefs.ts`.
- `[LATER]` Decided to exist, but not in the milestone being built.
- `[OPEN]` No default yet.

## 1. High Concept

A god-style observation simulation. The player sets an environment, places bot entities, and watches what the society does.

1. `[CONFIRMED]` The map is made of purposeful nodes. The starting purposes are resource, danger, and shelter; ruin (M7a) and water (M7b) were added later (section 4).
2. `[CONFIRMED]` Bots act on their own. They explore, use resources, and avoid danger. The player does not steer a bot.
3. `[CONFIRMED]` Bot goals are survival and expansion. Expansion happens because bots reproduce by themselves.
4. `[CONFIRMED]` The player observes, inserts nodes, and inserts bots, then sees how those changes affect the society.
5. `[CONFIRMED]` There is no score, win state, or player-versus-bot contest. The simulation is the product.

## 2. Design Pillars

- The environment is a set of purposeful nodes `[CONFIRMED]`.
- Bots act without direct control `[CONFIRMED]`.
- Bots survive and reproduce `[CONFIRMED]`.
- The player studies cause and effect `[CONFIRMED]`. Anything the player does must be traceable to a visible change in the society.
- Runs are deterministic `[CONFIRMED]`. Same seed plus same commands gives the same world, so an intervention can be compared against a control run.

## 3. Map and Time

1. `[CONFIRMED]` The map is a grid, about 96 x 64 cells. Positions are continuous inside it; nodes and bots have real-valued x and y.
2. `[CONFIRMED]` One run is one shared society on one map.
3. `[CONFIRMED]` Time advances in fixed ticks. Bots act in id order inside a tick, so the order is deterministic.
4. `[PROVISIONAL]` Tick order for one bot: perceive, memory, decide, act, metabolise, death check. After the bot loop, in this order: dead bots are removed, new children are added, the lineage store is pruned if it is over its cap (section 5, item 11), each dead bot leaves a ruin (in id order), resources regrow, ruins decay and the emptied ones are removed, world events and meteor craters whose time is up are removed, family knowledge is shared (section 5, item 10), and a stats sample is taken every N ticks. A plague hurts inside the bot loop (during the bot's metabolise step) and a drought or bounty acts in the regrow step; both are still in force on their last tick. Births, deaths and player commands go to the event log.

## 4. Environment Nodes

All nodes share one data shape (`WorldNode`). A node's `kind` decides which fields matter.

1. `[CONFIRMED]` **Resource**: bots eat it to gain energy. It depletes as it is eaten and regrows by a fixed amount per tick, never above its capacity. Built in M1.
2. `[CONFIRMED]` **Danger**: damages the health of any bot inside its radius, every tick. Bots remember it and avoid it. Built in M3.
3. `[CONFIRMED]` **Shelter**: heals bots inside it and slows their energy loss. It is the only place a bot can reproduce. Built in M3.
4. `[PROVISIONAL]` Radii, capacity, regrowth, damage, heal and energy-loss multiplier per kind (`src/content/nodeDefs.ts`).
5. `[PROVISIONAL]` Bots steer around a remembered danger (radius plus a 0.75 cell margin) with greedy turning, no path search. A bot inside a danger flees. A bot below 20% energy that is seeking food ignores avoidance and may walk into danger for food ("groves" in the eden preset put food inside danger zones). This is why danger deaths are rare but real.
6. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Ruin** (M7a, built): when a bot dies, from any cause, it leaves a small temporary food node at the place of death. It closes the loop (the dead feed the living) and leaves a visible trace where bots starve or die in danger.
   - Amount: 24 food units (`ruin.baseAmount`) plus half of the bot's remaining energy counted in food units (`ruin.energyShare` 0.5, energy / `energyPerUnit`). A bot that starved has none left and leaves 24; one that died of old age at 90 energy leaves 46. Because the base is a fixed gift, ruins add some food to the world, which is why the numbers were re-checked (see Open Questions).
   - Bots treat a ruin as food, using the same seek-and-eat path as a resource (same hunger rule, same danger avoidance; a desperate bot may walk into danger for it, and how early a bot gets desperate depends on its caution, item 3 of section 5). Two differences: a ruin never regrows, so a remembered ruin that held less than one bite is not retried later, and an emptied ruin is removed.
   - Decay: a ruin loses 0.05 units per tick (`nodeDefs.ruin.decay`), so an untouched 24-unit ruin lasts about 480 ticks. `[PROVISIONAL]` (M12) A ruin whose centre lies inside any danger node's radius (a danger zone, a grove's zone or a meteor crater) decays `ruin.dangerDecayMult` (5) times as fast, so about 100 ticks instead of 480 for a 24-unit ruin: it stays as a visible trace but stops luring starving bots into danger. No random draws. It is removed when it reaches 0, whether eaten or decayed. A bot heading for a removed ruin finds the node missing and wanders; memory drops removed nodes, as for any removed node.
   - Merging: a death within 2 cells of an existing ruin (`ruin.mergeRadius`) adds to it instead of making a new one, up to the ruin's capacity of 60 (`nodeDefs.ruin.capacity`; the excess is lost). At 60 ruins (`ruin.maxCount`) a death adds to the nearest ruin however far away. This count is separate from the player's `maxNodes` cap: ruins are not counted against it and never stop the player placing a node.
   - Ruins are made by the sim only. The player cannot place one (not in the toolbar, and a `placeNode` command with kind `ruin` is refused), but can inspect a ruin and remove it with the Remove tool (a recorded `removeNode` command, replay-safe).
   - Ruin creation uses no random draws and is not logged (one event per death would flood the log). Resource depletion is not logged any more either (see section 7).
7. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Water** (M7b, built): a permanent spring and the source of the second need, thirst (section 5, item 9).
   - A bot standing within a water node's radius (2 cells, `nodeDefs.water`) drinks `bot.drinkRate` (5) thirst per tick. The spring is never used up and has no amount: `amount` and `capacity` stay 0, and nothing is subtracted when a bot drinks, so any number of bots can drink at once. This is the simplest deterministic choice: no accounting, no regrowth, no ordering effects between bots.
   - Water is a normal player node: the Add water tool (key 3) places it (a recorded `placeNode` command, same 1-cell gap rule as other kinds, replay-safe) and the Remove tool takes it away. The map draws it as a droplet with the letter W in cyan, over a faint disc that shows where a bot can drink; the legend, toolbar badge, hover ghost, inspector (drink rate, "never runs dry", bots inside) and chart markers (W) all carry the letter and shape, never colour alone.
   - Eden has eight springs: four fixed at (14,44), (82,14), (36,54) and (78,48), plus four scattered by the seed. (78,48) is 6 cells from the south-east grove's centre, so its zone touches that danger zone's edge. The scattered four land near food or shelter on some seeds and nowhere in particular on others; "some near food, some near shelter" holds by chance, not by placement.
8. `[OPEN]` Further node purposes (disease, trade). Record them here when decided.

## 5. Bots

1. `[CONFIRMED]` Bots interact with each other only through shared nodes, with one exception: a parent and its own child share what they remember (item 10; user decision of 2026-11-09). There is no fighting, trading, or other direct contact between bots, and no sharing between siblings, other relatives or strangers.
2. `[CONFIRMED]` A bot senses nodes within a sense radius, and keeps a small memory of what it saw; that memory can also hold sightings copied from its parent or child (item 10), marked with who they came from. `[PROVISIONAL]` Memory holds about 8 entries, plus 2 extra slots used only by water (`memory.cap` 8, `memory.waterCap` 2); within each group the oldest sighting is forgotten first. Water has its own slots because springs are few and permanent while food, ruins and shelters crowd the memory and churn: with one shared cap a thirsty bot kept forgetting where it last drank. A remembered-empty resource is rechecked after a delay.
3. `[CONFIRMED]` Intent is one of wander, seek food, seek water, flee, rest, reproduce. All six are built (M3, M7b). `[PROVISIONAL]` The bot picks the intent with the highest utility score (`brain.ts`; weights in `tuning.ts` `brain`). Starvation can out-score fleeing, which is what makes a starving bot take risks. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Desperate** (M12): a bot seeking food (or water) below its desperate fraction ignores danger avoidance (it accepts food or a spring inside a remembered danger and does not steer around danger on the way). The fraction is `desperateBelow` (0.2) x (1 + `cautionDesperate` (4) x (0.5 - caution)), clamped to `minDesperate` (0.05) to `maxDesperate` (0.4), so a reckless bot (caution 0.3 gives 36% energy) takes risks early, a founder-caution bot at 20%, and a cautious one (caution 0.7 and up: 5%) starves before it walks into danger. Hunger and thirst share it; the same function is used by the intent choice and by movement (`desperateFraction` in `brain.ts`), and the numbers are in `tuning.brain`. This makes caution cost something (before M12 it cost nothing and mean caution drifted from 0.5 to about 0.3 over 60,000 ticks).
4. `[PROVISIONAL]` A bot starts looking for food below 60% energy and eats until about 90%. Eating takes a fixed amount per tick from the node.
5. `[PROVISIONAL]` Energy drains every tick, more on a tick the bot moves, scaled by its `metabolism` trait. A bot at 0 energy dies of starvation.
6. `[CONFIRMED]` Every bot carries `traits` (sense radius, caution, reproduction threshold, metabolism) from the first milestone.
7. `[PROVISIONAL]` Offspring inherit traits with small mutations (M6, built). Each trait of a newborn changes with chance `mutation.rate` (0.5) by a random factor within +-`mutation.strength` (10%), then is clamped into per-trait bounds (sense radius 3 to 12, caution 0.05 to 1.5, reproduction threshold 55 to 90, metabolism 0.7 to 1.6). All of it is seeded (the `birth` stream, eight extra draws per child), so runs stay deterministic. Founders and player-placed bots keep the default traits. Strength 0 makes children copy the parent.
8. `[PROVISIONAL]` Every trait has a cost, so selection finds a balance instead of pushing to a limit. Sense radius: each cell above the default 6 costs 0.02 energy per tick (a smaller radius saves it). Caution: a more cautious bot flees danger instead of seeking food near it, and (M12) holds off entering danger for food until it is closer to starving (item 3). Reproduction threshold: a low threshold breeds early, but the parent also burns 20 energy per birth (`birthCost`) on top of the 40% it hands over, so breeding on a thin reserve is risky; the upper bound is 90 because a bot stops eating at 90. Metabolism: scales energy loss and also walking speed (speed is `1 + 2 x (metabolism - 1)` times the base), so a slow bot saves energy but takes much longer to reach food. Since M7b a hotter metabolism also dries the bot out faster (item 9: thirst drain times `max(1, metabolism)` cubed). These are tuning hypotheses (`senseDrain`, `metabolismSpeed`, `birthCost`, `mutation` in `tuning.ts`).

9. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Thirst** (M7b, built): every bot has a thirst level from 0 to `bot.maxThirst` (100). It starts full for founders, player-placed bots and newborns alike (water, unlike energy, is not handed down from the parent). It falls by `bot.thirstDrain` (0.25) per tick, times `max(1, metabolism)` to the power `bot.thirstMetabolismPower` (3), so a founder goes from full to dead in exactly 400 ticks without water; a cooler bot (metabolism under 1) pays the same 0.25, and a hot one pays much more. Shelter does not slow it. Standing at a spring refills `drinkRate` (5) per tick. At 0 the bot dies of thirst.
   - The power of 3 is a tuning finding, not a design claim. Speed is `1 + 2 x (metabolism - 1)` times the base, so a hot bot is cheaper per cell walked and finds the few springs sooner. With thirst scaled by metabolism x 1 (or not at all), metabolism 1.6 out-bred 0.9 in `tests/traits.test.ts` once water existed, which broke the M6 rule that every trait has a cost. At 3 a hot bot's thirst (x4.1 at 1.6, x1.5 at 1.15) cancels that. The "only above 1" part is also a finding: scaling a cool bot's thirst down as well (power 1.5 to 3 over the whole range) pinned the population mean metabolism at the 0.7 lower bound over 60,000 ticks. With the floor it settles around 0.8 to 1.05.
   - A bot looks for water below 50% thirst (`thirstyBelow`) and keeps drinking until the pull of the spring (`thirstWeight` 1.25 times the missing thirst fraction) falls under the wander score, which is about 88% thirst, the same way eating stops near 88% energy. That is the same shape and weight as hunger, so a bot that is both hungry and thirsty goes for whichever need is lower. Ties go flee, food, water, rest, reproduce, wander.
   - Spring versus danger: a bot ignores a remembered spring that sits inside a remembered danger (plus the 0.75 cell margin) unless it is desperate, meaning thirst below the bot's desperate fraction (20% for a bot of founder caution, higher for a reckless one and lower for a cautious one, shared with hunger; see item 3 of section 5). A desperate water seeker also ignores danger avoidance on the way, the same bargain as food in a grove. With a spring touching a danger zone, a cautious bot detours or drinks elsewhere and a parched one takes the risk.
   - A bot that has never seen a spring simply wanders; sense radius and speed decide how soon it finds one. Reproduction does not depend on thirst (it stays shelter plus energy, adult age and cooldown).
10. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Family knowledge sharing** (M10, built). The rule behind items 1 and 2 is `[CONFIRMED]` (a user decision); the numbers are provisional. Only a parent and its own child share, so knowledge reaches siblings and grandparents by relay through a parent link, never directly. There are no player goals and no tribe or village concept. Code: `src/sim/family.ts`, numbers in `tuning.family`.
   - **At birth** (`family.inherit`, true): the child starts with a copy of the parent's memory as it was at conception, limited to the shared kinds. **By exchange**: on ticks where `tick % shareEvery === 0` (every 10 ticks), a living parent and child within `shareRadius` (3 cells) swap memory. A radius of 0 turns exchange off and `inherit: false` turns off the birth copy; both off is the old behaviour (`--no-family` in the CLI).
   - **What passes**: entries of kinds resource, water, shelter and danger (`shareKinds`). Not ruins: they are gone within a few hundred ticks, so hearing about one is rarely worth it.
   - **Merge and staleness**: an entry is copied only if the receiver lacks that node or holds a strictly older sighting. The copy keeps the original `seenTick` and `amount`, so how stale it is travels with it and the retry rule (`memory.retryTicks`) still applies: a remembered-empty resource is rechecked after 300 ticks whoever told you. Entries of nodes that no longer exist are skipped. Then the usual caps apply (8 plus 2 water slots, oldest sighting forgotten first), so a shared entry can only push out an older one, and is itself dropped if it is the oldest.
   - **Provenance**: `MemoryEntry.learnedFrom` is the id of the parent or child the entry came from, or null when the bot saw the node itself. Seeing the node again clears it. `World.familyLearned` counts entries learned (birth and exchange, counted after the trim); the survey prints it as `learned`.
   - **Order and determinism**: a new phase after the world events expire and before the stats sample. Bots are visited in id order and each checks only its own parent (a lookup, not a search), so the cost is linear in the population. Both sides of a pair merge from copies taken before either changes, so a pair is symmetric. A chain (A parent of B parent of C) is handled in id order and each pair mutates in turn, so C can already hear on the same tick what B just learned from A. No random numbers are drawn.
   - **Effect** (eden, seeds a..j, warmup 1,000, against `--no-family`, which reproduces the pre-M10 numbers exactly): thirst deaths fall by about 60% (1,079 to 443 over 20,000 ticks summed over the ten seeds, 4,433 to 1,825 over 60,000; thirst is 6% of deaths instead of 13 to 14%); starvation deaths are about the same over 60,000 ticks (24,217 against 24,302) and a little lower over 20,000; births are 7 to 9% lower; the population range is 19 to 76 over 20,000 ticks and 19 to 97 over 60,000, with a max/min swing of 1.5 or more on every seed. Mean evolved traits move a little (mean sense radius 5.08 to 4.94 and mean caution 0.39 to 0.34 over 60,000 ticks). The ruins' share of intake falls slightly (8.5% to 7.6%). A bot gets about 20 entries learned per birth over a run, mostly re-copies of fresher sightings. These are tuning hypotheses.

11. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Lineage records** (M11, built). Bookkeeping for a later family-tree view: dead bots leave `World.bots`, so `World.lineage` keeps one small record per bot that ever lived (id, parent id, generation, tick born, tick died and cause, children ever born, birth traits), in ascending id order. It lives in the world, so it replays and hashes with the run; a save (seed + commands) is unchanged. It draws no random numbers and nothing in the sim reads it, so population and trait numbers are identical to M10 for every seed (only the state hash changes). Code: `src/sim/lineage.ts`, numbers in `tuning.lineage`.
   - **Records**: founders and player-placed bots have no parent; a newborn's parent record counts one more child; a death sets `died` (the tick) and `cause`, for bot-loop deaths and for meteor deaths alike.
   - **Cap and pruning**: when there are more than `maxRecords` (3,000) records, they are pruned down to `pruneTo` (2,400) in one batch. First every record with a living descendant is marked. Then dead, unmarked records (extinct branches) go, earliest death first (ties by lowest id); if that is not enough, the oldest dead ancestors of living bots go (lowest id first). A living bot's own record is never dropped. A record whose parent record is gone is valid (a family view says "earlier ancestors not kept"). `World.lineagePruned` counts the dropped records.
   - **Queries** (pure, not yet used by the UI): `familyView` (ancestors nearest first, descendants as a size-limited tree with hidden-child counts, living and total descendant counts of the kept records) and `founderLines` (every record without a kept parent, with its living and total descendants).
   - **Cost** (eden, seeds a, b, 60,000 ticks): about 2,500 to 2,800 records kept and 1,202 pruned; the state JSON grows from about 410 KB to about 1 MB and `stateHash` from about 15 ms to about 45 ms. About 230 bytes per record; 3,000 records add roughly 0.7 MB. These are provisional numbers.

## 6. Reproduction and Death

1. `[CONFIRMED]` A bot reproduces alone. No partner is needed.
2. `[CONFIRMED]` Conditions: energy at or above the bot's reproduction threshold, adult, cooldown finished, and standing in a shelter. Built in M3.
3. `[PROVISIONAL]` The child takes 40% of the parent's energy (a transfer, not new energy), and the parent burns a further 20 energy (`birthCost`, lost, M6). A cooldown follows for parent and child. A hard cap of 2000 bots keeps the sim safe; births stop at the cap. The child inherits the parent's traits with a small mutation (section 5, item 7).
4. `[CONFIRMED]` Causes of death: starve, danger, age; `[PROVISIONAL]` thirst was added as a fourth (M7b). All four are built, checked in the order danger, thirst, starve, age. When more than one is true on the same tick the first in that order is the recorded cause: danger first because it is done to the bot, then the two needs (thirst before starve, the faster killer), then age. A death event records the cause and the place. A thirst death leaves a ruin like any other death.

## 7. Society and Events

1. `[CONFIRMED]` The sim keeps a stats sample every N ticks (population, running births and deaths, total food, and from M6 the population mean of each trait) and an event log of births, deaths and player commands (a ring buffer of about 500). `[PROVISIONAL]` Resource depletions are no longer logged (M7a): they filled the buffer within a few hundred ticks and pushed births, deaths and commands out of it. The chart's command markers and (UI only, `src/ui/logMerge.ts`) the event log's older command rows come from `World.commands`, which keeps every command, so no command scrolls out of the log; the other entry types are limited to the newest 500.
2. `[CONFIRMED]` Player actions are commands (place node, remove node, place bot) recorded with their tick, so a run can be replayed. Built in M4. `[PROVISIONAL]` A command is applied between two ticks (after tick T, before T+1) and recorded as `{tick: T, cmd}`; a refused command is not recorded. A placed node uses its kind's defaults and may not sit within 1 cell of a node of the same kind (different kinds may overlap, like the preset groves); the map holds at most 400 nodes; a placed bot uses the default traits and a heading from the `world` stream, so placing never shifts the `ai` draws. The UI snaps placements to cell centres. Removing a bot is not a command.
3. `[PROVISIONAL]` **World events** (M8, built): four things the player triggers to shake the society. They are player commands (`triggerEvent`), applied between ticks like a placement, recorded in `World.commands` with their tick, replay-safe (same `stateHash` as the live run), and a refused one is not recorded. No random draws are used when an event is applied. Rules live in `src/sim/worldEvents.ts`, numbers in `tuning.events`.
   - **Drought** (global, timed): resource regrowth is multiplied by 0.35 for 1,500 ticks. **Bounty** (global, timed): multiplied by 3 for 1,000 ticks. Springs never regrow and ruins never regrow, so neither is affected. A drought and a bounty running together multiply (x1.05). The same global event cannot start while it is running (refused with "already active", ticks left).
   - **Plague** (area, timed): the player clicks a cell; every bot within 14 cells loses 0.8 health per tick for 800 ticks, on top of any danger damage. Shelter healing (0.5) and the base regen (0.02) still apply, so a shelter does not cancel it (0.5 + 0.02 < 0.8) and only slows the loss. A bot at full health that stays dies in about 125 ticks. Bots do not perceive a plague: it is invisible to the brain on purpose (a new memory kind and avoidance would make it harmless to any bot that already left it, and bots would never wander in by accident). They only feel their health fall and may walk to a shelter to rest, as after any injury. Plague damage and danger damage are kept apart so a death can be told which it was.
   - **Meteor** (area, instant): the player clicks a cell; bots within 6 cells lose health at once, 200 at the centre falling in a straight line to 0 at the edge, so anything within 3 cells of the centre dies (100 health). Bots it kills die at that moment with cause `meteor` (death event logged, ruin left, all in id order). It leaves a crater: a danger node of radius 3 and 2 damage per tick (the same as a danger zone), which expires after 600 ticks. Bots remember it and steer around it through the ordinary danger paths (flee, avoid, desperate bots may cross). The crater is a normal `danger` node with an end tick (`WorldNode.expires`, 0 for permanent nodes); it is not counted against `player.maxNodes`, can be inspected (with its time left) and removed with the Remove tool.
   - **Caps**: at most 3 timed events (drought, bounty, plague) at once, so plagues share the cap, and at most 6 craters; a refused command says why ("Too many world events at once (3)").
   - **Death cause order** is now: health (danger or plague), thirst, starve, age. If health ran out and the bot took plague damage that tick, the cause is `plague` when the plague damage was larger than the danger damage that tick, otherwise `danger` (a tie goes to danger). `meteor` is only recorded by the strike itself, never by the tick check.
   - **Effect sizes** (eden, seeds a..j, event at tick 3000, mean population over the next 1,500 ticks; the plague and meteor aimed at the busiest spot, as a player would): control 42.2; drought 23.1 (-45%, lowest value 10 on any seed); bounty 78.1 (+85%); plague 32.5 (-23%, 12 bots killed per seed, lowest 11); meteor 40.0 (-5%, 3 bots killed per seed, lowest 25). An unaimed plague at the map centre costs only 5% and kills about 3 bots (bots are sparse there), a meteor on empty ground kills none. Events at tick 8000 give similar results (drought -45%, bounty +90%, plague -22%). These are tuning hypotheses (see Open Questions).
   - Event-free runs behave exactly as before M8 (same population, births and deaths per seed); only the state hash changes, because the world gained the `events` list, two death-cause counters and `WorldNode.expires`.
4. `[PROVISIONAL]` The observer UI (M5): KPI strip (tick, population with change arrow, births and deaths per minute, food), a population and food chart with a marker per player command, deaths by cause, a filterable event log whose entries select the bot or node in the inspector, and an inspector. A "minute" is 600 ticks (`tuning.stats.minuteTicks`), which is one minute at 1x speed. `[OPEN]` Whether this is enough for cause and effect (see Open Questions). `[PROVISIONAL]` The event log has Birth, Death and Command filters (all on). `[PROVISIONAL]` Food is shown as resource food: the KPI Food tile and the chart's food line count resources only, and the tile's detail line shows the food sitting in ruins separately (for example "+84 in ruins"), never added in. The map draws a ruin as a diamond with the letter U in violet (a resource is a circle, R), with a legend entry and an inspector view (amount, decay per tick, time left if untouched). `[PROVISIONAL]` Trait panels (M6): a "Traits over time" section with one small line chart per trait (current mean, drift since the first sample with a sign and arrow, the founders' value as a faint dotted line; distinct line style and end marker per trait), a "Population traits" table (mean, min, max, with a bar showing the spread inside the trait's allowed range), and the inspector shows how far the selected bot is from the population mean (+/-). `[PROVISIONAL]` World events in the UI (M8): a "World events" panel under the map with four cards (Drought D, Bounty B, Plague P, Meteor M), each with its key, how long it lasts and what it does; drought and bounty fire at once, plague and meteor arm a map tool (the hover ghost shows the radius and an X when it would be refused). A card is disabled with the reason written under it when the sim would refuse it. Below the cards an "In progress" list shows each running event or crater with its ticks left, a progress bar and what it does; a crater can be selected. On the map, drought and bounty are one-line chips along the top edge (letter badge, name, ticks left, and the effect on a map wider than 560px), a plague is a hatched disc with a dashed ring labelled "P PLAGUE" with the ticks left (shortened to "P 800", then "P", or left out, when the disc is too small for the text), and a crater is an orange dashed danger zone with an M and an expanding impact ring for its first 24 ticks. The population chart gets a marker letter per event (H drought, F bounty, P plague, M meteor; the existing R D S W B X are unchanged, and crowded markers still fold as "P+2") and a shaded band for each timed event over the ticks it was in force, labelled and patterned (diagonal lines drought, dots bounty, crosshatch plague). The event log has a World filter (the end of a drought, bounty or plague, and a crater fading; the start is the player's command) and says "died: plague" or "died: meteor"; Deaths by cause has Plague and Meteor rows. The inspector shows a crater as "Meteor crater" with its time left. Keys D, B, P, M do not clash with 1-7 and Esc. `[PROVISIONAL]` Water in the UI (M7b): the toolbar has seven tools with keys 1 to 7 in button order (Inspect 1, Add resource 2, Add water 3, Add danger 4, Add shelter 5, Add bot 6, Remove 7; Esc returns to Inspect), the bot inspector has a Thirst bar with a "low" flag under 20% (like Energy), memory shows "n / 10 nodes · k from family", a Family section lists the parent (id, alive or gone, a link when alive) and the living children (a count and links to the first four), and Deaths by cause has a Thirst row (cyan bar). The event log says "died: thirst" and the inspector "Died of thirst".
5. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Page layout** (UI polish stage, after M9). ## 8. Technology

1. `[CONFIRMED]` Vite 8, React 19, TypeScript ~6.0, Vitest 4, oxlint, a `tsx` CLI harness, Canvas 2D for the map, GitHub Pages hosting. Versions match `trading-sim`.
2. `[CONFIRMED]` Layers: `sim`, `content`, `harness`, `persist`, `ui`, with import rules enforced by `tests/architecture.test.ts`. The sim uses no `Math.random` and no `Date`; all randomness comes from the streams `world`, `ai` and `birth`.
3. `[CONFIRMED]` The world is plain JSON, so it can be saved, copied and hashed (`stateHash`).
4. `[PROVISIONAL]` Save and load (M9, built). A run is fully determined by its seed, preset and recorded commands (pillar: runs are deterministic), so a save holds only those, plus the tick reached and the state hash at that tick. Saves are tiny and a loaded run replays to the same `stateHash`. Code: `src/persist` (pure data, no content import) and `src/ui` (loading, panel).
   - **Format** (`src/persist/save.ts`), save text is JSON: `{ "version": 1, "seed", "presetId", "tick", "hash", "commands": [{ "tick", "cmd" }, ...] }`. `seed` is 1 to 32 letters, digits or dashes; `presetId` a short id (only `eden` exists); `tick` a whole number from 0 to 1,000,000 (the tick the run had reached); `hash` is 16 lowercase hex characters (`stateHash` at `tick`); `commands` are the accepted player commands in the order they were applied, each with the tick it was applied at (between tick T and T+1), at most 5,000, ticks never decreasing and never above the saved `tick`. A command is `placeNode` (kind resource, danger, shelter or water; x, y), `removeNode` (id), `placeBot` (x, y) or `triggerEvent` (event drought, bounty, plague or meteor; x and y optional). Key order inside a command matters, because the commands are part of the hashed state; the encoder and decoder keep the sim's own order. Unknown fields or command shapes are rejected, not ignored.
   - **Versioning**: `version` is the format number (1 today). A bigger number than the game knows is refused with "made by a newer version" (it does not guess). A new format bumps the number and an `upgradeSave` step converts older saves on read, so old files keep loading. Adding a new command type or node kind later is a content change plus a version bump, because an old game could not replay it.
   - **Validation**: `decodeSave` never throws. It limits the text to 1,000,000 characters, parses with `JSON.parse` only (nothing is evaluated), checks every field and returns a typed error (`not-json`, `not-a-save`, `unsupported-version`, `newer-version`, `bad-field`, `too-many-commands`, `bad-command`, `bad-order`, `too-large`) with a message a player can read. What needs content is checked at load: the preset must exist and the replay must accept every command (a refused command fails the load; a coordinate outside the map is one).
   - **Loading**: the replay runs in the simulation worker (item 5) in slices (200 ticks per step, at most about 40 ms per slice, `src/ui/replayJob.ts` driven by `simHost.ts`), so the page stays fully responsive, with a progress bar and a Cancel button (a message to the worker, which stops between two slices). The new world is built completely before it replaces the old one; a failed or cancelled load leaves the current run untouched. While a load runs the clock, the tools and a restart are stopped. A loaded run starts paused at the saved tick. If the replayed hash differs from the saved one the run still loads, with a warning that it was made by a different version of the simulation. A replay takes about as long as playing the same ticks (roughly 3,000 to 7,000 ticks a second in Chrome here, so 60,000 ticks is about 8 to 20 seconds; it ran on the page's thread before M13 and froze it in short bursts, now the page keeps drawing at its full frame rate).
   - **Ways to save**: Save to file (`garden-of-eden-<seed>-t<tick>.json`); Load from file (asks first if the current run has commands); Copy share link (the save as base64url in the URL fragment, `#run=...`; capped at 12,000 characters, about 150 commands, longer runs are saved to a file; opening a link loads that run paused at its tick, with no confirmation because opening a link is an explicit act, and the fragment is then removed from the address bar so a reload does not replace your run); autosave of the latest run in `localStorage` with a Resume last run button and a Clear button.
   - **Autosave**: written for a run that has a command or was started on purpose (restart or load), so opening the page never overwrites the last real run with the untouched starting world. A new command writes at once, a running clock at most every 10 seconds, and the page flushes when hidden or closed (the save and its state hash are made by the worker, so a write is a request that finishes a moment later and never stalls the page; a flush as the page closes is best effort). Clear removes it and stops saving the current run until it changes. Without browser storage the page works and says autosave is unavailable.
   - **Not in a save**: the speed, pause state, selected tool and selection (the world itself is rebuilt). Removing a bot is not a command and so is not saved (see section 7, item 2).
5. `[PROVISIONAL]` The simulation runs in a Web Worker (M13, built). Nothing about the rules changed: `step`, `applyCommand`, the replay and `stateHash` are the same code on the same data, only where it runs moved, and every state hash is identical (`npm run sim -- --seeds a,b,c --ticks 20000 --warmup 1000` prints the same rows as before).
   - **Where the logic lives** (`src/ui`, the ui layer may import sim and content; `src/sim` is still free of UI and DOM, so `tests/architecture.test.ts` needed no exception): `simHost.ts` is pure and DOM-free. `createSimHost()` owns the `World`, the replay (load) job and, from M15, the heat grid; `handle(msg)` takes one message and returns the replies, `pumpLoad()` runs one slice of a load. `createHostRunner()` wires a host to `postMessage` and a timer. `sim.worker.ts` is a 6-line adapter around it (no `webworker` lib in the TypeScript config, so `self` is typed locally). `simClient.ts` has `createWorkerClient()` (a real Worker; if `Worker` is missing, the script cannot load or it fails before it says `ready`, it becomes an inline client, replays the messages sent meanwhile and the page shows a warning toast) and `createInlineClient()` (the same host on the page's thread; Vitest uses it, with `structuredClone` on every message so nothing is shared). `protocol.ts` has the messages.
   - **Protocol**: to the worker `advance{ticks, slow?}`, `command{reqId, cmd}`, `reset{seed, presetId?}`, `load{save}`, `cancelLoad`, `exportSave{reqId}`, `select{id|null}`, `query{reqId, kind: 'family' | 'founders', ...}` (answers with `familyView` and `founderLines`; the tree view in M14 uses it), `heat{layer|null, range}` (reserved for M15; accepted and ignored) and `sync{reqId}`; from the worker `ready`, `frame{frame, ack?}`, `commandResult`, `loadProgress`, `loadDone`, `save`, `queryResult`, `synced` and `error`. Messages are handled strictly in order, so a command lands between the ticks of the advance before it and the one after it, at the right tick, and the replay of the recorded run still matches the live hash.
   - **Clock**: `loop.ts` and `planFrame` stay on the page. `advance` has at most one request in flight; ticks that fall due meanwhile pile up (capped at `MAX_TICKS_PER_FRAME`, 200) and go out together, so a slow worker slows the run instead of flooding its queue. The picture's progress between ticks (`alpha`) is applied with the frame that matches it, so bots never jump backwards; interpolation itself is unchanged.
   - **The page's copy** (`viewWorld.ts`): not the `World` but a `ViewWorld`. Every frame carries the tick, the nodes, the bots without their memory (`ViewBot`: `memoryCount` and `learnedCount` instead) and the small totals (events, births, deaths by cause, eaten, command count). The big lists (samples, log, commands, the selected bot with its full memory) are slow data: sent at most about four times a second, when paused or stepping, and with every answer to a command, a selection, a reset or a load. The lineage and (M15) the heat grid are only sent on request. Panels take a `ViewWorld`; `checkCommand` (the hover ghost and the greyed event cards) runs on the page against it. A frame is plain objects: at 64 bots it is about 35 KB and 0.4 ms to build and clone; at the 2,000-bot cap it would be about 800 KB and 12 ms, so typed arrays are an option if populations ever get that large (the eden preset holds about 25 to 80).
   - **Store API** (`store.ts`): `dispatch` returns a `Promise<CommandResult>`, `exportSave` a `Promise<Save>`, `advance` and `stepOnce` a promise that resolves when their ticks are shown, `startLoad` and `cancelLoad` go through messages (a cancel resolves the load as cancelled when the worker confirms), `settled()` resolves when every earlier message has been answered (tests use it). `resetWorld` still returns a boolean at once, and the new world appears with the worker's first frame. `queryFamily` and `queryFounders` wrap the queries. All the load rules in item 4 hold unchanged.
6. `[PROVISIONAL]` The family panel (M14, built) lets the player study lineages. It reads the lineage records through the worker's `query` message (`queryFamily`, `queryFounders` in `store.ts`) and changes no sim rule, number or hash.
   - **What it shows**: for the selected bot (or any record picked in the panel) the record's status, three counters (living descendants, total descendants, deepest generation), the ancestors as a chain (nearest first, 5 by default, "+n older" asks for up to 40, "Earlier ancestors not kept" when the lineage was pruned) and the descendants as an open-and-close tree (3 levels open by default, at most 12 children per node and 200 rows, then "+n more"). With nothing selected it shows the founder lines (founders, bots you placed, and in a long run the oldest line whose ancestors were pruned) with living and total descendants, most living first.
   - **How it behaves**: a living bot's row selects it on the map and in the inspector; a dead bot's row (or an ancestor chip, or a founder line) points the panel at that record, which shows its born and died ticks, cause, parent and birth traits. "Focus" re-centres the tree on any row. The inspector has "Show family tree" for the selected bot (and for a dead one). Status is always text first (alive, or "died at t 12,345 · Starved") with a glyph as a second cue. The tree is plain HTML for keyboard and screen reader use: arrow keys move, right opens or goes to the first child, left closes or goes to the parent, Home and End jump, one row at a time is in the tab order. On a narrow panel the indentation stops after 4 levels and deeper rows show "+n deeper".
   - **Data flow**: the panel asks again when the focus changes and about once a second while the run is going (a paused run only if the tick moved), keeps the last answer on screen while the next one is on its way, and ignores answers that arrive after a newer request. The tree box has a fixed height and scrolls, so a refresh moves nothing. The side column (`.area-side`) has `contain: size`, so a tall side column can no longer stretch the grid rows and push the map down.

## 10. Open Questions

- Cross-engine hashes (found while testing M13): a 60,000-tick run replayed in Node (the CLI) and in Chrome ends with different state hashes (seed `big60k`, 3 commands: Node `b7b31408e2ba12f3`, Chrome `7509699adbd01983`), so a save made by one engine loads in the other with the "different version of the simulation" warning. The same file gives the same warning in the pre-M13 app, so the worker did not cause it, and a save made in the browser reloads in the browser with a matching hash. The cause was not investigated (a likely suspect is a `Math` function such as `hypot`, `sin` or `cos` that is not bit-exact across V8 builds); it only matters if saves are to be exchanged between the CLI and the browser, or between browsers. Is that worth chasing?
- Lineage (M11): are 3,000 records (pruned to 2,400) the right cap? A 60,000-tick eden run makes 1,900 to 4,100 births, so about half the seeds prune; a pruned tree loses its extinct branches and its oldest ancestors, so the founders' lines fade after a long run. Hashing the state is about 45 ms with the store full; since M13 it happens in the worker, so autosave no longer stalls the page. The family panel (M14) reads these records through the worker's `query` message; the founder-lines table is where a pruned run shows up (lines starting at a high generation).
- Family panel (M14): is the tree too busy on a long run (a line can have hundreds of descendants; only 12 children per node and 200 rows are listed, ranked by living descendants)? Would a search by bot id, or a view of the whole forest, help? "+n older" is an upper bound (it counts generations, and pruned records make the real chain shorter). Below about 300 px wide the tree box scrolls sideways a little.
- Family sharing (M10): is the effect what the user wants? It removes about 60% of thirst deaths (knowledge of springs travels down a family line) and shifts mean evolved caution down a little, but it is mostly invisible on the map. Is the dotted family line plus the inspector rows enough to see it, or is a lineage view (the planned M11) or a stat such as "share of memory learned from family" needed? Are the numbers right (`shareRadius` 3, `shareEvery` 10)? If a run ever became too stable, the retune order is: raise `shareEvery`, shrink `shareRadius`, drop `resource` from `shareKinds`, inherit only. Saves made before M10 load with the different-version warning (the state gained `learnedFrom` and `familyLearned`); a simulation version in the save would let the warning say which side is newer (see the Saves question).
- Saves (M9): a long run's share link does not fit (about 150 commands), so a file or the autosave is the only way to keep it. Is a compressed link worth it (the browser's `CompressionStream` is async and would roughly halve the link)? Should a save also keep the pause state, speed or selection, and should a shared link carry a note or title? A save made before a rule or tuning change loads with a hash warning and may play out differently: should a save carry a simulation version so the warning can say which side is newer?

- Which extra node purposes are needed beyond water (for example disease, trade)?
- Are drought, bounty, plague and meteor (M8) the right set, and are the sizes right? A drought halves the population on average and the lowest value across seeds was 10 of about 42; an aimed plague costs about a quarter; a meteor costs about 3 bots and is mostly a local, visible strike (a bigger radius or damage would make it matter more). Should bots perceive and avoid a plague (see section 7 item 3)? Should events stack or exclude each other (a drought and a bounty together multiply today)? Further events (disease spread, migration, a flood) are not designed.
- Is the M5 observer UI enough for cause and effect? Old player commands no longer scroll out of the event log (it lists them from `World.commands`); births, deaths and world-event ends are still limited to the newest 500, so an old death can leave the log. Is that enough?
- Is there any player goal beyond observation and intervention?
- Numbers: the eden preset (40 bots, 34 food nodes plus 3 groves, 4 danger, 12 shelter, 8 springs) with ruins and thirst holds 24 to 74 bots across seeds a..j after warmup over 20,000 ticks (21 to 82 over 60,000, never extinct). That is the post-retune figure; the numbers below it in this paragraph are from before the retune. Ruins supply about 5 to 9% of the food eaten over 20,000 ticks and 7 to 10% over 60,000. Thirst deaths are 10 to 29% of all deaths per seed over 20,000 ticks and 8 to 22% over 60,000 (starvation is still 55 to 80%), and danger deaths stay rare (0 to 11 per 20,000 ticks, 0 to 58 per 60,000). Water cut the population against the pre-water numbers (27 to 76 and 27 to 89), mostly at the top end. Is that the feel the user wants (swings are about 2x, and the founders still all die of age together near tick 3000)? The old open point that ruins make a danger zone tempting for a desperate bot was handled in M12 (ruins in danger decay 5x faster, and caution sets how early a bot is desperate); see the M12 changelog entry for the numbers.
- Traits (M6, re-checked after M7b): over 60,000 ticks the reproduction threshold falls from 80 to about 57 to 63 on every seed (breeding early pays), metabolism stays near its founder value (means 0.79 to 1.03), sense radius wanders (4.3 to 6.8), and caution varies a lot between seeds (0.26 to 0.82, measured before M12; since M12 the 60,000-tick means are 0.46 to 0.86 on every seed, mean 0.60, because caution now has a cost: item 3 of section 5). Water made a plain `metabolism x thirst` cost too weak (a fast bot found springs sooner than it burned energy), and a cost that also rewarded slow bots pinned them to the lower bound, hence `max(1, metabolism)` cubed (item 9 of section 5). Are those the trait stories the user wants to watch, or should the costs be tuned to keep more variety? Sense radius is not rewarded more strongly by water: a radius of 12 still loses to 6 in the selection test.
