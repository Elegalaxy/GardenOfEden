## How to read this document

Every rule carries a status tag. Do not treat a rule as decided unless it says `[CONFIRMED]`.

- `[CONFIRMED]` The user decided or approved this. Changing it needs a new user decision.
- `[PROVISIONAL]` A default that is built or planned but not settled. Numbers in particular are tuning hypotheses, not balance claims. They live in `src/content/tuning.ts` and `src/content/nodeDefs.ts`.
- `[LATER]` Decided to exist, but not in the milestone being built.
- `[OPEN]` No default yet.

## 1. High Concept

A god-style observation simulation. The player sets an environment, places bot entities, and watches what the society does.

1. `[CONFIRMED]` The map is made of purposeful nodes. The starting purposes are resource, danger, and shelter; ruin (M7a) and water (M7b) were added later (section 4). In M21b (user decision of 2026-11-16) the player-placed danger zone became the predator (section 4 item 2b); the internal `danger` kind remains only for meteor craters.
2. `[CONFIRMED]` Bots act on their own. They explore, use resources, and avoid danger. The player does not steer a bot.
3. `[CONFIRMED]` Bot goals are survival and expansion. Expansion happens because bots reproduce by themselves.
4. `[CONFIRMED]` The player observes, inserts nodes, and inserts bots, then sees how those changes affect the society.
5. `[CONFIRMED]` There is no score, win state, or player-versus-bot contest. The simulation is the product.

## 2. Design Pillars

- The environment is a set of purposeful nodes `[CONFIRMED]`.
- Bots act without direct control `[CONFIRMED]`.
- Bots survive and reproduce `[CONFIRMED]`.
- The player studies cause and effect `[CONFIRMED]`. Anything the player does must be traceable to a visible change in the society.
- Runs are deterministic `[CONFIRMED]`. Same seed plus same commands gives the same world, so an intervention can be compared against a control run.

## 3. Map and Time

1. `[CONFIRMED]` The map is a grid, about 120 x 80 cells in Eden and Blank (user decision of 2026-11-23, map120; it was 96 x 64 before `SIM_VERSION` 16). A scripted or test scene may use any size. Positions are continuous inside it; nodes and bots have real-valued x and y.
2. `[CONFIRMED]` One run is one shared society on one map.
3. `[CONFIRMED]` Time advances in fixed ticks. Bots act in id order inside a tick, so the order is deterministic.
4. `[PROVISIONAL]` Tick order for one bot: perceive, memory, decide, act, metabolise, death check. After the bot loop, in this order: dead bots are removed, new children are added, the lineage store is pruned if it is over its cap (section 5, item 11), each dead bot leaves a ruin (in id order), resources regrow, ruins decay and the emptied ones are removed, world events and meteor craters whose time is up are removed, family knowledge is shared (section 5, item 10), and a stats sample is taken every N ticks. With two sexes (M17, section 6 item 3b) the bot loop only collects the bots that are ready to breed; mates are paired after the dead are removed, just before the children are added. A plague hurts inside the bot loop (during the bot's metabolise step) and a drought or bounty acts in the regrow step; both are still in force on their last tick. Births, deaths and player commands go to the event log.

## 4. Environment Nodes

All nodes share one data shape (`WorldNode`). A node's `kind` decides which fields matter.

1. `[CONFIRMED]` **Resource**: bots eat it to gain energy. It depletes as it is eaten and regrows each tick by its base amount, scaled by the season (and by any drought or bounty), never above its capacity. Built in M1; the season factor came with S2 (section 4 item 8).
2. `[CONFIRMED]` **Danger** (internal kind `danger`): damages the health of any bot inside its radius, every tick. Bots remember it and avoid it. Built in M3. Since M21b it exists only as the meteor crater (the player cannot place one; `placeNode` of kind `danger` is refused with "Danger zones were replaced by predators.").
2b. `[CONFIRMED]` **Predator** (M21b, user decision of 2026-11-16): a moving hunter that replaces the placeable danger zone; its mechanics are in section 8 item 10. The Add predator tool takes the old danger tool's slot (key 4). Eden starts with five (since map120, `SIM_VERSION` 16: the three old groves scaled to the 120 x 80 map plus a south-west and a north-east grove), and Blank with none. `[PROVISIONAL]` (E1 mechanics, switched on in Eden by E2, `SIM_VERSION` 10; user decision reverses the M21b "fixed in number" choice) With `predator.ecology` on, predators eat (energy from kills), starve, die of age and breed alone into free territory; each starting or placed predator marks a den, an empty den is re-colonised from the map edge after `recolonizeTicks`, and removing a predator removes its den. Meteors and plague do not hurt predators. `[PROVISIONAL]` (P1 mechanics, switched on in Eden by P2, `SIM_VERSION` 12) With `predator.prey` on, the number of predators follows the number of bots: the limit is one predator per 16 bots (never below 1, so they cannot die out this way, nor above `maxCount`); over the limit cubs and newcomers wait, and after 500 ticks over it the lowest-energy predator that the player did not place leaves the map. Player-placed predators never leave. `[PROVISIONAL]` (H1 mechanics, switched on in Eden by H2, `SIM_VERSION` 13; user decisions: traits only, no sexes; the "no sexes" part was reversed by PS1 below) With `predator.traits` on, a cub inherits its parent's sense (reach), speed and stamina (chase length before giving up) with a seeded mutation (rate 0.5, strength 0.2), clamped to bounds (sense 0.75 to 1.5, speed 0.8 to 1.2, stamina 0.5 to 2); founders, placed and arriving predators start at 1 and a cub is one generation after its parent. Each trait costs a little energy drain (prices 0.15, 0.3, 0.06), so no predator is simply best. `[PROVISIONAL]` (PS1 mechanics, switched OFF, user decision of 2026-11-21; this REVERSES the H1 decision "traits only, no sexes"; PS2 retune sweep: no setting met every bar, so it stays OFF, see the changelog) With `predator.sexes` on (and ecology on), a predator is female or male: every new predator takes the sex there are fewer of (a tie gives female; no draw), so Eden's founders are female, male, female, and an arrival brings the missing sex. Only a female bears a cub, after `killsPerCub` 3 kills, and only with an adult male within `mateRange` 120 of her (that is the map's whole width; since map120 the diagonal is longer, so a far male can be out of range; the male pays nothing and has no cooldown); with no mate there is no cub. If one sex has been missing for `loneAfter` 3000 ticks any predator breeds alone as before and its cub is the missing sex (accepted lone fallback). A cub's traits cross over from both parents (each trait from the mother or the father, then the usual mutation) and its generation is the higher parent's plus 1. The prey rule never sends away the only predator of its sex unless nobody else can leave. The Add predator tool gets an F/M picker in S3. Numbers are first guesses, not retuned (that is PS2).
3. `[CONFIRMED]` **Shelter**: heals bots inside it and slows their energy loss. It is the only place a bot can reproduce. Built in M3.
4. `[PROVISIONAL]` Radii, capacity, regrowth, damage, heal and energy-loss multiplier per kind (`src/content/nodeDefs.ts`).
5. `[PROVISIONAL]` Bots steer around a remembered danger (radius plus a 0.75 cell margin) with greedy turning, no path search. A bot inside a danger flees. A bot below 20% energy that is seeking food ignores avoidance and may walk into danger for food (since M21b the "groves" of the eden preset are food nodes with a predator that makes its home there). Predator deaths are rare but real (about 5% of deaths).
6. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Ruin** (M7a, built): when a bot dies, from any cause, it leaves a small temporary food node at the place of death. It closes the loop (the dead feed the living) and leaves a visible trace where bots starve or die in danger.
   - Amount: 24 food units (`ruin.baseAmount`) plus half of the bot's remaining energy counted in food units (`ruin.energyShare` 0.5, energy / `energyPerUnit`). A bot that starved has none left and leaves 24; one that died of old age at 90 energy leaves 46. Because the base is a fixed gift, ruins add some food to the world, which is why the numbers were re-checked (see Open Questions).
   - Bots treat a ruin as food, using the same seek-and-eat path as a resource (same hunger rule, same danger avoidance; a desperate bot may walk into danger for it, and how early a bot gets desperate depends on its caution, item 3 of section 5). Two differences: a ruin never regrows, so a remembered ruin that held less than one bite is not retried later, and an emptied ruin is removed.
   - Decay: a ruin loses 0.05 units per tick (`nodeDefs.ruin.decay`), so an untouched 24-unit ruin lasts about 480 ticks. `[PROVISIONAL]` (M12) A ruin whose centre lies inside any danger node's radius (since M21b only a meteor crater; a predator does not speed decay) decays `ruin.dangerDecayMult` (5) times as fast, so about 100 ticks instead of 480 for a 24-unit ruin: it stays as a visible trace but stops luring starving bots into danger. No random draws. It is removed when it reaches 0, whether eaten or decayed. A bot heading for a removed ruin finds the node missing and wanders; memory drops removed nodes, as for any removed node.
   - Merging: a death within 2 cells of an existing ruin (`ruin.mergeRadius`) adds to it instead of making a new one, up to the ruin's capacity of 60 (`nodeDefs.ruin.capacity`; the excess is lost). At 60 ruins (`ruin.maxCount`) a death adds to the nearest ruin however far away. This count is separate from the player's `maxNodes` cap: ruins are not counted against it and never stop the player placing a node.
   - Ruins are made by the sim only. The player cannot place one (not in the toolbar, and a `placeNode` command with kind `ruin` is refused), but can inspect a ruin and remove it with the Remove tool (a recorded `removeNode` command, replay-safe).
   - Ruin creation uses no random draws and is not logged (one event per death would flood the log). Resource depletion is not logged any more either (see section 7).
7. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Water** (M7b, built): a permanent spring and the source of the second need, thirst (section 5, item 9).
   - A bot standing within a water node's radius (2 cells, `nodeDefs.water`) drinks `bot.drinkRate` (5) thirst per tick. The spring is never used up and has no amount: `amount` and `capacity` stay 0, and nothing is subtracted when a bot drinks, so any number of bots can drink at once. This is the simplest deterministic choice: no accounting, no regrowth, no ordering effects between bots.
   - Water is a normal player node: the Add water tool (key 3) places it (a recorded `placeNode` command, same 1-cell gap rule as other kinds, replay-safe) and the Remove tool takes it away. The map draws it as a droplet with the letter W in cyan, over a faint disc that shows where a bot can drink; the legend, toolbar badge, hover ghost, inspector (drink rate, "never runs dry", bots inside) and chart markers (W) all carry the letter and shape, never colour alone.
   - Eden has eight springs: four fixed at (14,44), (82,14), (36,54) and (78,48), plus four scattered by the seed. (78,48) is 6 cells from the south-east grove's centre, so its zone touches the threat radius of the predator at that grove. The scattered four land near food or shelter on some seeds and nowhere in particular on others; "some near food, some near shelter" holds by chance, not by placement.
8. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Seasons** (S1 mechanics, S2 switched ON in Eden, `SIM_VERSION` 9, save format unchanged; user-approved design of 2026-11-16, so section 4 item 1 was reworded). Resource regrowth only swings with the tick: the regrowth multiplier is `base + amplitude * sin(2 * pi * tick / period)` (`tuning.seasons`: `enabled` true, `period` 3000, `amplitude` 0.4, `base` 1.02, the S2 retune, see the changelog; S1's first guesses were 4000, 0.3, 1; amplitude may not exceed 0.5, and `seasonAt` refuses a period of 0 or less). Tick 0 is the start of spring, so spring starts at the base, summer starts a quarter period in and peaks, autumn starts at half a period (the base again, falling), winter starts at three quarters and bottoms out; the four names are the four quarters. `base` exists because a node that is already full wastes the summer surplus, so a base of 1 gives less food over a year than the old fixed rate: if intake drops by more than 5%, raise base to 1.05 before lowering amplitude. The multiplier multiplies with a drought or bounty (a drought in winter is 0.35 times the winter low). Water, ruins, fertility, brains and memory are unchanged and bots are unaware of the season (a remembered amount that has gone stale is handled by the existing retry); the player has no control over it. No random draws. With `enabled` false the multiplier is the literal 1, so worlds, samples and saves gain no field and every hash is identical to before. Code: `src/sim/seasons.ts` (`seasonAt`), `regrow` in `nodes.ts`; the harness flags `--no-seasons` and `--set path=value`. `[CONFIRMED]` item 1 above was reworded in S2 with the user's approval ("regrows each tick by its base amount, scaled by the season (and by any drought or bounty)").
9. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Rough ground** (R1, mechanics only, switched OFF, user-approved 2026-11-20; `SIM_VERSION` 15 and the save format unchanged, zero hash change with it off). A permanent node kind `rough`: a disc (radius 6 by default, a preset may give its own) where walking is slow. A mover whose step starts inside any rough disc takes a step `terrain.botSpeedMult` (bots) or `terrain.predatorSpeedMult` (predators) times as long (0.5 each for now, separate numbers); overlapping discs do not compound. Slow only: no extra energy cost, and bots do not perceive it (it is never remembered or avoided). No random draws. `Preset.terrain` lists the discs; Eden has a marsh belt, layout A (60,4), (61,14), (59,34), (60,44), made only while `terrain.enabled` (layout B, patches, is kept as a comment in `presets.ts` for R2). The player tool comes in R3 (rough nodes count toward `player.maxNodes` and have their own cap `terrain.maxCount`, 24; `checkCommand` already refuses a placement while off or at the cap). See `src/sim/terrain.ts`. R2c (user-approved, 2026-11-24, still OFF): a switch `terrain.wanderOnly` (default false, last in the terrain object; hashes with it off are unchanged) slows a bot on rough ground only on a wander step; walks to food, water, shelter or a mate and flee steps keep full speed; predators unchanged.
9b. `[OPEN]` Further node purposes (disease, trade). Record them here when decided.

## 5. Bots

1. `[CONFIRMED]` Bots interact with each other only through shared nodes, with two exceptions: a parent and its own child share what they remember (item 10; user decision of 2026-11-09), and since M17 bots perceive each other **for mating only** (section 6 item 3b; user decision of 2026-11-13): a ready male senses ready females within his sense radius and walks to the nearest. `[PROVISIONAL]` (D1, user decision of 2026-11-20, an exception to this rule) Disease spreads by contact: a bot that stays within `disease.contactRadius` (3 cells since D2) of a sick bot for `catchAfter` (5) ticks falls sick too (section 7 item 3). Since D2 it is on, and it starts only when the player triggers an outbreak (nothing happens in a world without one). `[PROVISIONAL]` (C1, user decision of 2026-11-20, a second exception to this rule; mechanics only, switched OFF) Elders teach: every `culture.teachEvery` (20) ticks a young bot (age under `youngAge` 600 x longevity) takes one lesson from the nearest elder (age from `elderAge` 1800 x longevity) within `teachRadius` (3 cells) that is not its own mother or father (family sharing covers those; grandparents and strangers may teach). What is taught is PLACES ONLY: at most `perLesson` (2) water and shelter memory entries (`teachKinds`), freshest first, copied only where the pupil lacks the node or knows an older sighting. Elders learn nothing in this phase. Section 5 item 10 (no tribe) is untouched. There is no fighting, trading, or other direct contact between bots, and (apart from these elder lessons) no sharing between siblings, other relatives, strangers, or the two parents with each other.
2. `[CONFIRMED]` A bot senses nodes within a sense radius, and keeps a small memory of what it saw; that memory can also hold sightings copied from its parent or child (item 10), marked with who they came from. `[PROVISIONAL]` Memory holds about 8 entries, plus 2 extra slots used only by water (`memory.cap` 8, `memory.waterCap` 2); within each group the oldest sighting is forgotten first. Water has its own slots because springs are few and permanent while food, ruins and shelters crowd the memory and churn: with one shared cap a thirsty bot kept forgetting where it last drank. A remembered-empty resource is rechecked after a delay.
   - `[PROVISIONAL]` **Directional sensing** (V1 mechanics, V2 retune and switch-on, on in Eden: botFront 1, botRear 0.7, predatorFront 1, predatorRear 0.35, trackTicks 10; `tuning.perception.cone`). Reach depends on the angle off the bot's heading: radius x (`botRear` + (`botFront` - `botRear`) x (1 + cos) / 2), so a bot sees farthest ahead and only a short way behind (anything within radius x `botRear` is always seen). A predator seen by the bot itself stays tracked for `trackTicks` after it leaves the cone while it is within the front reach; sightings drop only by time. Predators get the same cone (`predatorFront`, `predatorRear`) on their heading and keep a lock on the bot they chase. No draws; with front = rear = 1 or the cone off the world is as before. V3 (UI) is built: the selected bot or predator shows its sight as a dashed outline, and the Inspector has a Sight row.
3. `[CONFIRMED]` Intent is one of wander, seek food, seek water, flee, rest, reproduce. All six are built (M3, M7b). `[PROVISIONAL]` The bot picks the intent with the highest utility score (`brain.ts`; weights in `tuning.ts` `brain`). Starvation can out-score fleeing, which is what makes a starving bot take risks. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Desperate** (M12): a bot seeking food (or water) below its desperate fraction ignores danger avoidance (it accepts food or a spring inside a remembered danger and does not steer around danger on the way). The fraction is `desperateBelow` (0.2) x (1 + `cautionDesperate` (4) x (0.5 - caution)), clamped to `minDesperate` (0.05) to `maxDesperate` (0.4), so a reckless bot (caution 0.3 gives 36% energy) takes risks early, a founder-caution bot at 20%, and a cautious one (caution 0.7 and up: 5%) starves before it walks into danger. Hunger and thirst share it; the same function is used by the intent choice and by movement (`desperateFraction` in `brain.ts`), and the numbers are in `tuning.brain`. This makes caution cost something (before M12 it cost nothing and mean caution drifted from 0.5 to about 0.3 over 60,000 ticks).
4. `[PROVISIONAL]` A bot starts looking for food below 60% energy and eats until about 90%. Eating takes a fixed amount per tick from the node.
5. `[PROVISIONAL]` Energy drains every tick, more on a tick the bot moves, scaled by its `metabolism` trait. A bot at 0 energy dies of starvation.
6. `[CONFIRMED]` Every bot carries `traits` (sense radius, caution, reproduction threshold, metabolism) from the first milestone. `[PROVISIONAL]` Since M22 it carries four more (sprint, longevity, hardiness, wariness; item 8b), eight in all.
7. `[PROVISIONAL]` Offspring inherit traits with small mutations (M6, built). Since M17 each trait of a newborn is first taken from its mother or its father (one `birth` draw per trait, uniform crossover), then changes with chance `mutation.rate` (0.5) by a random factor within +-`mutation.strength` (10%), then is clamped into per-trait bounds (sense radius 3 to 12, caution 0.05 to 1.5, reproduction threshold 55 to 90, metabolism 0.7 to 1.6, and since M22 sprint 0.8 to 1.8, longevity 0.7 to 1.4, hardiness 0.7 to 1.6, wariness 0.5 to 2). All of it is seeded (the `birth` stream; 28 draws per sexual child since M22: angle, distance, heading, sex, eight crossover, sixteen mutation; 19 per asexual child; it was 16 and 11 with four traits), so runs stay deterministic. Founders and player-placed bots keep the default traits. Strength 0 makes children copy the parent.
8. `[PROVISIONAL]` Every trait has a cost, so selection finds a balance instead of pushing to a limit. Sense radius: each cell above the default 6 costs 0.02 energy per tick (a smaller radius saves it). Caution: a more cautious bot flees danger instead of seeking food near it, and (M12) holds off entering danger for food until it is closer to starving (item 3). Reproduction threshold: a low threshold breeds early, but the mother also burns 10 energy per birth (`birthCost`; 20 before M17) on top of the 40% she hands over, so breeding on a thin reserve is risky; the upper bound is 90 because a bot stops eating at 90. Metabolism: scales energy loss and also walking speed (speed is `1 + 2 x (metabolism - 1)` times the base), so a slow bot saves energy but takes much longer to reach food. Since M7b a hotter metabolism also dries the bot out faster (item 9: thirst drain times `max(1, metabolism)` cubed). These are tuning hypotheses (`senseDrain`, `metabolismSpeed`, `birthCost`, `mutation` in `tuning.ts`).

8b. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Four more traits** (M22, built; SIM_VERSION 8; save format unchanged). They are appended to `TRAIT_NAMES` (`sprint`, `longevity`, `hardiness`, `wariness`), so the older traits keep their draw order. Founders and placed bots start every one at 1 (not at a bound, since mutation is multiplicative). Each has a trade-off; numbers are in `tuning.ts` (`bot.sprintUpkeep`, `hardinessDrain`, `longevityDrain`, `mutation.bounds`).
   - **Sprint** (0.8 to 1.8): a fleeing bot moves `sprint` times its normal speed (`movement.ts` `flee`; wandering, seeking and mating are unchanged). Price: `sprintUpkeep` (0.01) x (sprint - 1) energy per tick, times metabolism (below 1 it saves, like sense radius), and a step taken while the intent is flee costs `moveDrain` x sprint. A predator moves at 0.9 and a bot at 0.6, so a sprint of 1.5 matches it.
   - **Longevity** (0.7 to 1.4): the bot's `lifespan` is round(`maxAge` x longevity x (1 - `lifespanLoss` x inbreeding)) (`Bot.lifespan` is set at birth), and the whole fertility curve scales with it (`fertilityAt(age, tuning, scale)` multiplies `adultAge`, `primeAge`, `declineAge` and `fertileUntil` by the bot's longevity; used by the breeding gate, both cooldowns, the inspector age stage and fertility row and the map's child size). A long-lived bot lives longer but matures and breeds later. Price: the energy drain is also multiplied by 1 + `longevityDrain` (0.03) x (longevity - 1).
   - **Hardiness** (0.7 to 1.6): every health damage (danger or crater, plague, predator bite in `metabolise`, meteor strike in `worldEvents.ts`) is divided by it. Price: the energy drain is multiplied by 1 + `hardinessDrain` (0.01) x (hardiness - 1).
   - **Wariness** (0.5 to 2): the threat radius of a remembered predator (`knownDangers` returns a copy with the radius times wariness; craters are not scaled; memory itself is untouched) and the predator memory window (`predator.memoryTicks` x wariness, in `remember`). It widens the zone a bot flees from, steers around and keeps food and water out of, for longer. Its price is behavioural only: food, water and shelter near a remembered haunt become off limits sooner.
   - **Finding (see the retune log in the changelog):** predator bites are only about 5% of deaths, so no anti-predator trait is selected hard. The drain prices have to be tiny: in a lineage race (mutation off, 16 seeds, 5,000 ticks) a hardiness of 1.4 at a drain of 0.1 left about half the descendants of a plain bot even with no predators. At the chosen prices the means over 60,000 ticks sit inside the bounds, not at them. Comparing 24 Eden seeds with and without predators at 40,000 ticks, sprint is a little higher with predators (1.015 against 0.988), but wariness is lower (0.89 against 0.98) and longevity is higher (1.12 against 1.08), the opposite of what was hoped; differences this size are within the seed noise. Wariness on its own is a net cost on Eden (the groves' food sits at the predators' homes), so it sinks below 1 where predators exist; it is not redundant with caution (caution scales how hard a bot flees and how early it gets desperate, wariness scales how far the danger reaches), but the user may want to drop it or change what its cost is.
9. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Thirst** (M7b, built): every bot has a thirst level from 0 to `bot.maxThirst` (100). It starts full for founders, player-placed bots and newborns alike (water, unlike energy, is not handed down from the parent). It falls by `bot.thirstDrain` (0.25) per tick, times `max(1, metabolism)` to the power `bot.thirstMetabolismPower` (3), so a founder goes from full to dead in exactly 400 ticks without water; a cooler bot (metabolism under 1) pays the same 0.25, and a hot one pays much more. Shelter does not slow it. Standing at a spring refills `drinkRate` (5) per tick. At 0 the bot dies of thirst.
   - The power of 3 is a tuning finding, not a design claim. Speed is `1 + 2 x (metabolism - 1)` times the base, so a hot bot is cheaper per cell walked and finds the few springs sooner. With thirst scaled by metabolism x 1 (or not at all), metabolism 1.6 out-bred 0.9 in `tests/traits.test.ts` once water existed, which broke the M6 rule that every trait has a cost. At 3 a hot bot's thirst (x4.1 at 1.6, x1.5 at 1.15) cancels that. The "only above 1" part is also a finding: scaling a cool bot's thirst down as well (power 1.5 to 3 over the whole range) pinned the population mean metabolism at the 0.7 lower bound over 60,000 ticks. With the floor it settles around 0.8 to 1.05.
   - A bot looks for water below 50% thirst (`thirstyBelow`) and keeps drinking until the pull of the spring (`thirstWeight` 1.25 times the missing thirst fraction) falls under the wander score, which is about 88% thirst, the same way eating stops near 88% energy. That is the same shape and weight as hunger, so a bot that is both hungry and thirsty goes for whichever need is lower. Ties go flee, food, water, rest, reproduce, wander.
   - Spring versus danger: a bot ignores a remembered spring that sits inside a remembered danger (plus the 0.75 cell margin) unless it is desperate, meaning thirst below the bot's desperate fraction (20% for a bot of founder caution, higher for a reckless one and lower for a cautious one, shared with hunger; see item 3 of section 5). A desperate water seeker also ignores danger avoidance on the way, the same bargain as food in a grove. With a spring touching a danger zone, a cautious bot detours or drinks elsewhere and a parched one takes the risk.
   - A bot that has never seen a spring simply wanders; sense radius and speed decide how soon it finds one. Reproduction does not depend on thirst (it stays shelter plus energy, adult age and cooldown).
10. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Family knowledge sharing** (M10, built). The rule behind items 1 and 2 is `[CONFIRMED]` (a user decision); the numbers are provisional. Only a parent and its own child share, so knowledge reaches siblings and grandparents by relay through a parent link, never directly. There are no player goals and no tribe or village concept. Code: `src/sim/family.ts`, numbers in `tuning.family`.
   - **At birth** (`family.inherit`, true): the child starts with a copy of its mother's memory as it was at conception, limited to the shared kinds, and then its father's memory is merged in (M17: a node the child lacks is added, a strictly newer sighting wins, copies are marked learned from the father). **By exchange**: on ticks where `tick % shareEvery === 0` (every 10 ticks), a living child swaps memory with its living mother and then its living father when within `shareRadius` (3 cells). The two parents never share with each other. A radius of 0 turns exchange off and `inherit: false` turns off the birth copy; both off is the old behaviour (`--no-family` in the CLI).
   - **What passes**: entries of kinds resource, water, shelter and danger (`shareKinds`). Not ruins: they are gone within a few hundred ticks, so hearing about one is rarely worth it.
   - **Merge and staleness**: an entry is copied only if the receiver lacks that node or holds a strictly older sighting. The copy keeps the original `seenTick` and `amount`, so how stale it is travels with it and the retry rule (`memory.retryTicks`) still applies: a remembered-empty resource is rechecked after 300 ticks whoever told you. Entries of nodes that no longer exist are skipped. Then the usual caps apply (8 plus 2 water slots, oldest sighting forgotten first), so a shared entry can only push out an older one, and is itself dropped if it is the oldest.
   - **Provenance**: `MemoryEntry.learnedFrom` is the id of the parent or child the entry came from, or null when the bot saw the node itself. Seeing the node again clears it. `World.familyLearned` counts entries learned (birth and exchange, counted after the trim); the survey prints it as `learned`.
   - **Order and determinism**: a new phase after the world events expire and before the stats sample. Bots are visited in id order and each checks only its own parent (a lookup, not a search), so the cost is linear in the population. Both sides of a pair merge from copies taken before either changes, so a pair is symmetric (a child has up to two pairs, mother first). A chain (A parent of B parent of C) is handled in id order and each pair mutates in turn, so C can already hear on the same tick what B just learned from A. No random numbers are drawn.
   - **Effect** (eden, seeds a..j, warmup 1,000, against `--no-family`, which reproduces the pre-M10 numbers exactly): thirst deaths fall by about 60% (1,079 to 443 over 20,000 ticks summed over the ten seeds, 4,433 to 1,825 over 60,000; thirst is 6% of deaths instead of 13 to 14%); starvation deaths are about the same over 60,000 ticks (24,217 against 24,302) and a little lower over 20,000; births are 7 to 9% lower; the population range is 19 to 76 over 20,000 ticks and 19 to 97 over 60,000, with a max/min swing of 1.5 or more on every seed. Mean evolved traits move a little (mean sense radius 5.08 to 4.94 and mean caution 0.39 to 0.34 over 60,000 ticks). The ruins' share of intake falls slightly (8.5% to 7.6%). A bot gets about 20 entries learned per birth over a run, mostly re-copies of fresher sightings. These are tuning hypotheses.

11. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Lineage records** (M11, built). Bookkeeping for a later family-tree view: dead bots leave `World.bots`, so `World.lineage` keeps one small record per bot that ever lived (id, parent id (the mother), father id and sex since M17, generation, tick born, tick died and cause, children ever born, birth traits), in ascending id order. It lives in the world, so it replays and hashes with the run; a save (seed + commands) is unchanged. It draws no random numbers and nothing in the sim reads it, so population and trait numbers are identical to M10 for every seed (only the state hash changes). Code: `src/sim/lineage.ts`, numbers in `tuning.lineage`.
   - **Records**: founders and player-placed bots have no parent; a newborn's mother and father records each count one more child; a death sets `died` (the tick) and `cause`, for bot-loop deaths and for meteor deaths alike.
   - **Cap and pruning**: when there are more than `maxRecords` (3,000) records, they are pruned down to `pruneTo` (2,400) in one batch. First every record with a living descendant is marked (walking up both the mother and the father line since M17). Then dead, unmarked records (extinct branches) go, earliest death first (ties by lowest id); if that is not enough, the oldest dead ancestors of living bots go (lowest id first). A living bot's own record is never dropped. A record whose parent record is gone is valid (a family view says "earlier ancestors not kept"). `World.lineagePruned` counts the dropped records.
   - **Queries** (pure, used by the family panel): `familyView` (the mother line as the ancestors, nearest first; the father; descendants as a size-limited tree with hidden-child counts, living and total descendant counts of the kept records) and `founderLines` (every record none of whose parents is kept, with its living and total descendants). Since M17 the family is a graph, not a tree: a child is listed under both its mother and its father, appears once in a view's tree (under the first parent reached) and is counted once in a view's totals, and founder lines can overlap (a child of two lines counts in both, so the line totals can add up to more than the records kept).
   - **Cost** (eden, seeds a, b, 60,000 ticks): about 2,500 to 2,800 records kept and 1,202 pruned; the state JSON grows from about 410 KB to about 1 MB and `stateHash` from about 15 ms to about 45 ms. About 230 bytes per record; 3,000 records add roughly 0.7 MB. These are provisional numbers.

## 6. Reproduction and Death

1. `[CONFIRMED]` Since M17 a birth needs two parents, a female and a male (user decision of 2026-11-13; before M17 a bot reproduced alone, which is still available as `tuning.sex.enabled` false, `--asexual` in the CLI). See item 3b.
2. `[CONFIRMED]` Conditions, for each of the two parents: energy at or above the bot's reproduction threshold, fertile, cooldown finished, and standing in a shelter. Built in M3. `[PROVISIONAL]` "Adult" became "fertile" in M16 (item 3a).
3. `[PROVISIONAL]` The child takes 40% of the mother's energy (a transfer, not new energy), and the mother burns a further 10 energy (`birthCost`, lost, M6; 20 before M17). A cooldown follows for the parents and the child. A hard cap of 2000 bots keeps the sim safe; births stop at the cap. The child inherits the parent's traits with a small mutation (section 5, item 7).
3a. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Aging fertility** (M16, built). Fertility is a piecewise linear curve of age (`tuning.fertility`, `fertilityAt` in `reproduction.ts`): 0 below `adultAge` (200), 50% (`startFraction`) at 200 rising to 100% at `primeAge` (600), 100% until `declineAge` (1,800), falling to 0 at `fertileUntil` (2,600), 0 after (`maxAge` 3,000 still ends the life). A bot can breed only while fertility is above 0, and after a birth its cooldown is `reproCooldown` (100 since M17, 300 before) divided by its fertility at that moment, rounded up (a 50% fertile parent waits 600 ticks). No random draws. The neutral setting (startFraction 1, primeAge = adultAge, declineAge = fertileUntil = maxAge) reproduces the pre-M16 world exactly (`tests/fertility.test.ts`). A newborn still starts with a full `reproCooldown`.
3b. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Two sexes** (M17, built; SIM_VERSION 3). Every bot is female or male (`Bot.sex`; lineage records keep it too). Founders alternate female, male, ... in creation order, so eden starts with 20 and 20 (no random draw). A newborn is female with chance `sex.femaleChance` (0.5), one `birth` draw: a plain coin flip (a `balance` term that pulled the ratio back was tried and rejected by the user). A bot you place gets whichever sex there are fewer of among the living (a tie gives female, no draw; the command itself is unchanged).
   - **Finding a mate** (`mating.ts`, `brain.ts`, `movement.ts`; no random draws): a bot is *ready* when it meets item 2 (energy, fertile, cooldown). A ready female goes to a shelter (the `reproduce` intent) and waits there. A ready male who senses a ready female within his own sense radius (the existing `senseRadius` trait) gets the `seekMate` intent (score `brain.seekMateScore` 0.6, above `reproduceScore` 0.5, so sight beats the trip to a shelter) and walks toward the nearest one (ties: lowest id), stopping within half the mating range; with no female in sight he heads for a shelter like any ready bot. A fresh grid of the ready females is built at the start of each tick (cells of 4), and a male scans only the cells his sense circle touches, so the cost does not grow with the population. Positions are the tick-start positions; bots are visited in id order. Females never search (a design choice: halving the walkers keeps the search cheap and gives the waiting females a place, the shelter, where a male can expect to find them).
   - **Mating** (`pairMates` in `reproduction.ts`, after the bot loop, dead removed, population cap respected): the **female must be standing in a shelter** and the ready male within `sex.mateRange` (4 cells) of her; the male does not need to be in a shelter himself (he has walked to her; requiring both inside a shelter made the meeting depend on where he stopped). Every such female-male pair is ranked by distance (ties: lowest female id, then lowest male id) and matched greedily, each bot at most once per tick, so a female takes the nearest free male and when two females want one male the nearer wins. A lone bot, or only one sex, never breeds.
   - **Costs**: the mother pays as in item 3 and waits `reproCooldown` / fertility. The father pays `sex.maleCost` (0, so nothing at present) energy and waits `sex.maleCooldown` (50) / fertility. The child appears by the mother, takes the *mother* as `parentId` and the father as `fatherId`, and its generation is one more than the older parent's. Traits: one `birth` draw per trait picks the mother's or the father's value, then the usual mutation. 16 `birth` draws per child (angle, distance, heading, sex, four crossover, eight mutation).
   - **Retune** (eden, seeds a..j; see the changelog): `eatRate` 5 to 10, `reproCooldown` 300 to 100, `birthCost` 20 to 10, founders' `reproThreshold` 80 to 65, `maleCost` 0, `maleCooldown` 50. Only half the population bears children and a male must find a female, so everything that gates a birth had to get easier. `eatRate` is the one that is furthest from the plan (5): at 5 or 8 the weakest seeds died out, alone or after a drought or plague, because births could not keep up with starvation at low density. With the mate search the planned `mateRange` of 4 works.
3c. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Inbreeding penalty** (M18, built; SIM_VERSION 4; user decision of 2026-11-13, "option 2": a penalty, no bar). There is no rule against pairing relatives, but a child of close relatives pays for it. At the pairing the sim scores the relation of the two parents from the lineage records (`relatedness` in `lineage.ts`, no random draws): **1** when one parent is the other's parent, or they are full siblings (same mother and same father); **0.5** for half siblings (exactly one parent in common); **0** for everyone else, cousins included. Ids are compared as stored in the two bots' own records, so a pruned ancestor does not hide a sibling pair; a parent that is null (founders, placed bots, asexual births) never matches anything, and with sexes off there is no father and so no score. The child stores the score (`Bot.inbreeding`, `LineageRecord.inbreeding`) and at score s starts with health cut by `healthLoss` x s and energy cut by `energyLoss` x s (the energy the mother paid stays paid), and gets its own `Bot.lifespan` = round(`bot.maxAge` x (1 - `lifespanLoss` x s)); the old-age death check uses that lifespan, and fertility is unchanged. Numbers in `tuning.inbreeding`: lifespanLoss 0.2, energyLoss 0, healthLoss 0.2 (the plan's start guess of 0.3 each was too harsh: scripted-event runs on some seed died out; see the changelog). `World.inbredBirths` counts births with a score above 0 (survey column `inb`). UI: the inspector shows Lifespan and Inbred rows, the family panel flags an inbred focus.

    **Mate choice (G1 mechanics, switched ON in Eden by G2, `SIM_VERSION` 14; user-approved design: a preference with fallback, but the sweep showed only no fallback meets the bar).** `tuning.mateChoice` = `{ enabled: true, avoidAt: 0.5, lastResort: false }` (G1 default was `enabled: false, lastResort: true`; the mechanics support both). With it on, kin means `kinship` (`src/sim/kin.ts`, the same 1 / 0.5 / 0 score as `relatedness` but from the two bots' own parent ids) of at least `avoidAt`, so half siblings count; only parents and siblings are kin. A ready male then senses the nearest ready female who is not kin and falls back to the nearest kin female only with `lastResort`; `pairMates` sorts candidate pairs by (kin, distance, female id, male id), so unrelated pairs form first and a kin pair forms only when both bots are still free, never with `lastResort` false. No random draws. Off, both paths run exactly as before.
4. `[CONFIRMED]` Causes of death: starve, danger (since M21b only a meteor crater), predator (M21b; before that only `[PROVISIONAL]` M21a mechanics), age; `[PROVISIONAL]` thirst was added as a fourth (M7b). All four are built, checked in the order danger, thirst, starve, age. When more than one is true on the same tick the first in that order is the recorded cause: danger first because it is done to the bot, then the two needs (thirst before starve, the faster killer), then age. A death event records the cause and the place. A thirst death leaves a ruin like any other death. `[PROVISIONAL]` M21a adds a sixth cause, `predator` (item 10 of section 8): it is recorded when health runs out and the predator bite damage that tick was strictly larger than both the danger and the plague damage (a tie goes to the older rules, so with no predator nothing changes), and it leaves a ruin like any other death. Since M21b `danger` means meteor craters only (shown as "Crater").

## 7. Society and Events

1. `[CONFIRMED]` The sim keeps a stats sample every N ticks (population, running births and deaths, total food, and from M6 the population mean of each trait) and an event log of births, deaths and player commands (a ring buffer of about 500). `[PROVISIONAL]` Resource depletions are no longer logged (M7a): they filled the buffer within a few hundred ticks and pushed births, deaths and commands out of it. The chart's command markers and (UI only, `src/ui/logMerge.ts`) the event log's older command rows come from `World.commands`, which keeps every command, so no command scrolls out of the log; the other entry types are limited to the newest 500.
2. `[CONFIRMED]` Player actions are commands (place node, remove node, place bot) recorded with their tick, so a run can be replayed. Built in M4. `[PROVISIONAL]` A command is applied between two ticks (after tick T, before T+1) and recorded as `{tick: T, cmd}`; a refused command is not recorded. A placed node uses its kind's defaults and may not sit within 1 cell of a node of the same kind (different kinds may overlap, like the preset groves); the map holds at most 400 nodes; a placed bot uses the default traits, the sex there are fewer of (M17) and a heading from the `world` stream, so placing never shifts the `ai` draws. The UI snaps placements to cell centres. Removing a bot is not a command.
3. `[PROVISIONAL]` **World events** (M8, built): four things the player triggers to shake the society. They are player commands (`triggerEvent`), applied between ticks like a placement, recorded in `World.commands` with their tick, replay-safe (same `stateHash` as the live run), and a refused one is not recorded. No random draws are used when an event is applied. Rules live in `src/sim/worldEvents.ts`, numbers in `tuning.events`. `[PROVISIONAL]` Events hurt predators (EP1 mechanics, switched ON in Eden by EP2: `tuning.events.predatorHarm` = `{ enabled true, meteorKillFraction 0.5, plagueDrain 0.4 }`; user-approved: a plague drains predator energy rather than killing outright, and a meteor kills predators in its core). With it on and predator ecology on, a meteor removes every predator within `meteor.radius * meteorKillFraction` of the impact (placed ones included, id order, dens kept in step) and a plague takes `plagueDrain` energy a tick from each predator inside each plague disc (stacking). Only predators with a life are touched, so fixed predators are not. Deaths are logged `struck` or `plagued` and counted in `ecology.stats.eventDeaths`. No random draws.
   - **Drought** (global, timed): resource regrowth is multiplied by 0.35 for 1,500 ticks. **Bounty** (global, timed): multiplied by 3 for 1,000 ticks. Springs never regrow and ruins never regrow, so neither is affected. A drought and a bounty running together multiply (x1.05). The same global event cannot start while it is running (refused with "already active", ticks left).
   - **Plague** (area, timed): the player clicks a cell; every bot within 14 cells loses 0.8 health per tick for 800 ticks, on top of any danger damage. Shelter healing (0.5) and the base regen (0.02) still apply, so a shelter does not cancel it (0.5 + 0.02 < 0.8) and only slows the loss. A bot at full health that stays dies in about 125 ticks. Bots do not perceive a plague: it is invisible to the brain on purpose (a new memory kind and avoidance would make it harmless to any bot that already left it, and bots would never wander in by accident). They only feel their health fall and may walk to a shelter to rest, as after any injury. Plague damage and danger damage are kept apart so a death can be told which it was.
   - **Blight** (area, timed, B1, user-approved 2026-11-20): the player clicks a cell; every resource node whose centre is within 12 cells (the same inside test as plague) stops regrowing and loses 0.25 food a tick (floored at 0) for 1,200 ticks, so a full node of 100 empties in 400 ticks. Several may run at once (drains add) and count against `events.maxActive`; the spot must be inside the map. Springs, ruins, shelters, craters, rough ground and predators are untouched, bots are not hurt, a node's toxin is untouched, and a drought or bounty gives no regrowth back inside the disc. Bots do not perceive it: they learn a node is empty the ordinary way (empty-node memory). Rules in `nodes.ts` `regrow`; numbers `tuning.events.blight` `{ duration 1200, radius 12, drain 0.25 }`; harness `--event blight@4000:48,32`. No random draws; event-free runs hash as before (`SIM_VERSION` 15 and `SAVE_VERSION` 3 unchanged; an older build refuses a save or link that contains a blight command as a bad command). UI (B2): tool key L, a fifth card and a stippled olive zone on the map.
   - **Outbreak** (area, instant, D1 mechanics, switched ON and tuned in D2, user-approved 2026-11-20; `tuning.disease`): the player clicks a cell; every living bot within 6 cells (`outbreakRadius`) falls sick at once, in id order. A sick bot takes 0.7 health damage a tick (`sickDrain`, divided by hardiness like every other health damage) for 200 ticks (`sickTicks`), then is immune for 1,500 ticks (`immuneTicks`) and can catch it again afterwards. The disease spreads by contact (the user-approved exception to section 5 item 1): a healthy bot within 3 cells (`contactRadius`) of a sick bot gains 1 dose a tick and falls sick at 5 (`catchAfter`); with no contact its dose falls by 1 a tick. A bot made sick this tick spreads it from the next tick; immune bots are not exposed; newborns start healthy and predators are unaffected. A bot that dies with the sickness as the strictly largest health damage dies of the new visible cause `disease`. It uses no random draws, is not a timed event (it sits in no `World.events` entry) and is refused while disease is off ("Disease is switched off.") or outside the map. State: `Bot.illness` (last key, absent until touched, deleted when it clears) and `World.disease` {infected, recovered} (created at the first outbreak, last key), so a run with no outbreak hashes the same with disease on or off. Section 5 item 10 (no tribe) is untouched. Code: `src/sim/disease.ts`, `--event outbreak@T:x,y`, `--no-disease`, survey columns `sick inf rec dis`; the tool, key, map drawing and card are D3. D2 (2026-11-20) tuned and switched it on: see the changelog. `[PROVISIONAL]` (R1 resistance, mechanics only, switched OFF; `tuning.resistance`) Heritable disease resistance, a latent gene: nothing exists until the first outbreak; then every living bot gets `resist` (1 to 1.3 at first, from its own seeded stream) and newborns inherit it (one parent's value, a small mutation, bounds 1 to 2). A higher resist raises the dose needed to fall sick (catchAfter x (1 + catchWeight x (resist - 1))) and shortens sickness (sickTicks / (1 + sickWeight x (resist - 1))); `upkeep` (0) is an optional energy cost per point above 1. No tribe, no new contact rule. Code: `src/sim/resistance.ts`, `--no-resistance`, `--set resistance.enabled=true`, survey columns `res0 res resMax`. Not tuned yet (R2 is the sweep).
   - **Meteor** (area, instant): the player clicks a cell; bots within 6 cells lose health at once, 200 at the centre falling in a straight line to 0 at the edge, so anything within 3 cells of the centre dies (100 health). Bots it kills die at that moment with cause `meteor` (death event logged, ruin left, all in id order). It leaves a crater: a danger node of radius 3 and 2 damage per tick (the same as a danger zone), which expires after 600 ticks. Bots remember it and steer around it through the ordinary danger paths (flee, avoid, desperate bots may cross). The crater is a normal `danger` node with an end tick (`WorldNode.expires`, 0 for permanent nodes); it is not counted against `player.maxNodes`, can be inspected (with its time left) and removed with the Remove tool.
   - **Caps**: at most 3 timed events (drought, bounty, plague) at once, so plagues share the cap, and at most 6 craters; a refused command says why ("Too many world events at once (3)").
   - **Death cause order** is now: health (danger or plague), thirst, starve, age. If health ran out and the bot took plague damage that tick, the cause is `plague` when the plague damage was larger than the danger damage that tick, otherwise `danger` (a tie goes to danger). `meteor` is only recorded by the strike itself, never by the tick check.
   - **Effect sizes** (eden, seeds a..j, event at tick 3000, mean population over the next 1,500 ticks; the plague and meteor aimed at the busiest spot, as a player would): control 42.2; drought 23.1 (-45%, lowest value 10 on any seed); bounty 78.1 (+85%); plague 32.5 (-23%, 12 bots killed per seed, lowest 11); meteor 40.0 (-5%, 3 bots killed per seed, lowest 25). An unaimed plague at the map centre costs only 5% and kills about 3 bots (bots are sparse there), a meteor on empty ground kills none. Events at tick 8000 give similar results (drought -45%, bounty +90%, plague -22%). These are tuning hypotheses (see Open Questions).
   - Event-free runs behave exactly as before M8 (same population, births and deaths per seed); only the state hash changes, because the world gained the `events` list, two death-cause counters and `WorldNode.expires`.

## 8. Technology

1. `[CONFIRMED]` Vite 8, React 19, TypeScript ~6.0, Vitest 4, oxlint, a `tsx` CLI harness, Canvas 2D for the map, GitHub Pages hosting. Versions match `trading-sim`.
2. `[CONFIRMED]` Layers: `sim`, `content`, `harness`, `persist`, `ui`, with import rules enforced by `tests/architecture.test.ts`. The sim uses no `Math.random` and no `Date`; all randomness comes from the streams `world`, `ai`, `birth`, `gene` and `predator` (the fourth, `gene`, was added in M20 with the user's approval on 2026-11-16 for the cosmetic bloodline colour; it is expanded after the other three, so their sequences for a seed are unchanged; the fifth, `predator`, was added in M21a with the user's approval, expanded after `gene` the same way, and it takes no draws while the world has no predator).
3. `[CONFIRMED]` The world is plain JSON, so it can be saved, copied and hashed (`stateHash`).
4. `[PROVISIONAL]` Save and load (M9, built). A run is fully determined by its seed, preset and recorded commands (pillar: runs are deterministic), so a save holds only those, plus the tick reached and the state hash at that tick. Saves are tiny and a loaded run replays to the same `stateHash`. Code: `src/persist` (pure data, no content import) and `src/ui` (loading, panel).
   - **Format** (`src/persist/save.ts`), save text is JSON: `{ "version": 2, "seed", "presetId", "sim", "tick", "hash", "commands": [{ "tick", "cmd" }, ...] }`. `seed` is 1 to 32 letters, digits or dashes; `presetId` a short id (`eden` or `blank`); `sim` the simulation (rules) version that made the save, a whole number from 1 to 1,000,000 (`SIM_VERSION` in `src/content/version.ts`, bumped whenever a rules or tuning change alters hashes); `tick` a whole number from 0 to 1,000,000 (the tick the run had reached); `hash` is 16 lowercase hex characters (`stateHash` at `tick`); `commands` are the accepted player commands in the order they were applied, each with the tick it was applied at (between tick T and T+1), at most 5,000, ticks never decreasing and never above the saved `tick`. A command is `placeNode` (kind resource, danger, shelter or water; x, y), `removeNode` (id), `placeBot` (x, y) or `triggerEvent` (event drought, bounty, plague or meteor; x and y optional). Key order inside a command matters, because the commands are part of the hashed state; the encoder and decoder keep the sim's own order. Unknown fields or command shapes are rejected, not ignored.
   - **Versioning**: `version` is the format number (2 today; version 1 had no `sim` and is upgraded to `sim` 1). A bigger number than the game knows is refused with "made by a newer version" (it does not guess). A new format bumps the number and an `upgradeSave` step converts older saves on read, so old files keep loading. Adding a new command type or node kind later is a content change plus a version bump, because an old game could not replay it.
   - **Validation**: `decodeSave` never throws. It limits the text to 1,000,000 characters, parses with `JSON.parse` only (nothing is evaluated), checks every field and returns a typed error (`not-json`, `not-a-save`, `unsupported-version`, `newer-version`, `bad-field`, `too-many-commands`, `bad-command`, `bad-order`, `too-large`) with a message a player can read. What needs content is checked at load: the preset must exist and the replay must accept every command (a refused command fails the load; a coordinate outside the map is one).
   - **Loading**: the replay runs in the simulation worker (item 5) in slices (200 ticks per step, at most about 40 ms per slice, `src/ui/replayJob.ts` driven by `simHost.ts`), so the page stays fully responsive, with a progress bar and a Cancel button (a message to the worker, which stops between two slices). The new world is built completely before it replaces the old one; a failed or cancelled load leaves the current run untouched. While a load runs the clock, the tools and a restart are stopped. A loaded run starts paused at the saved tick. If the replayed hash differs from the saved one the run still loads, with a warning that it was made by a different version of the simulation (worded "older" or "newer", with both rules versions, from the save's `sim` against the current one). A replay takes about as long as playing the same ticks (roughly 3,000 to 7,000 ticks a second in Chrome here, so 60,000 ticks is about 8 to 20 seconds; it ran on the page's thread before M13 and froze it in short bursts, now the page keeps drawing at its full frame rate).
   - **Ways to save**: Save to file (`garden-of-eden-<seed>-t<tick>.json`); Load from file (asks first if the current run has commands); Copy share link (the save JSON compressed with raw deflate, the browser's `CompressionStream`, and written as base64url in the URL fragment, `#run2=...`; capped at 64,000 characters of link, which holds the full 5,000-command save in ordinary play, while a longer or noisier run is saved to a file; links made before this change, `#run=...` (plain base64url, no compression), still open, and the plain form is only written when compression is unavailable or not shorter; an unpacked payload may not pass 1,000,000 bytes, so a tiny link cannot expand into a huge save; measured on realistic runs: 5 commands 244 characters (plain 522), 150 commands 1,950 (13,264), 1,000 commands 10,627 (87,379), 5,000 commands 50,642 (420,923), about 8 times shorter on long runs; a fragment is never sent to the server; opening a link loads that run paused at its tick, with no confirmation because opening a link is an explicit act, and the fragment is then removed from the address bar so a reload does not replace your run); autosave of the latest run in `localStorage` with a Resume last run button and a Clear button.
   - **Autosave**: written for a run that has a command or was started on purpose (restart or load), so opening the page never overwrites the last real run with the untouched starting world. A new command writes at once, a running clock at most every 10 seconds, and the page flushes when hidden or closed (the save and its state hash are made by the worker, so a write is a request that finishes a moment later and never stalls the page; a flush as the page closes is best effort). Clear removes it and stops saving the current run until it changes. Without browser storage the page works and says autosave is unavailable.
   - **Not in a save**: the speed, pause state, selected tool and selection (the world itself is rebuilt). Removing a bot is not a command and so is not saved (see section 7, item 2).
5. `[PROVISIONAL]` The simulation runs in a Web Worker (M13, built). Nothing about the rules changed: `step`, `applyCommand`, the replay and `stateHash` are the same code on the same data, only where it runs moved, and every state hash is identical (`npm run sim -- --seeds a,b,c --ticks 20000 --warmup 1000` prints the same rows as before).
   - **Where the logic lives** (`src/ui`, the ui layer may import sim and content; `src/sim` is still free of UI and DOM, so `tests/architecture.test.ts` needed no exception): `simHost.ts` is pure and DOM-free. `createSimHost()` owns the `World`, the replay (load) job and, from M15, the heat grid; `handle(msg)` takes one message and returns the replies, `pumpLoad()` runs one slice of a load. `createHostRunner()` wires a host to `postMessage` and a timer. `sim.worker.ts` is a 6-line adapter around it (no `webworker` lib in the TypeScript config, so `self` is typed locally). `simClient.ts` has `createWorkerClient()` (a real Worker; if `Worker` is missing, the script cannot load or it fails before it says `ready`, it becomes an inline client, replays the messages sent meanwhile and the page shows a warning toast) and `createInlineClient()` (the same host on the page's thread; Vitest uses it, with `structuredClone` on every message so nothing is shared). `protocol.ts` has the messages.
   - **Protocol**: to the worker `advance{ticks, slow?}`, `command{reqId, cmd}`, `reset{seed, presetId?}`, `load{save}`, `cancelLoad`, `commands{reqId, cmds}` (M19, see item 8), `exportSave{reqId}`, `select{id|null}`, `query{reqId, kind: 'family' | 'founders', ...}` (answers with `familyView` and `founderLines`; the tree view in M14 uses it), `heat{layer|null, range}` (which heatmap layer to send, M15; see item 7) and `sync{reqId}`; from the worker `ready`, `frame{frame, ack?}`, `commandResult`, `loadProgress`, `loadDone`, `save`, `queryResult`, `synced`, `error` and (M15) `heatSnapshot{heat}`. Messages are handled strictly in order, so a command lands between the ticks of the advance before it and the one after it, at the right tick, and the replay of the recorded run still matches the live hash.
   - **Clock**: `loop.ts` and `planFrame` stay on the page. `advance` has at most one request in flight; ticks that fall due meanwhile pile up (capped at `MAX_TICKS_PER_FRAME`, 200) and go out together, so a slow worker slows the run instead of flooding its queue. The picture's progress between ticks (`alpha`) is applied with the frame that matches it, so bots never jump backwards; interpolation itself is unchanged.
   - **The page's copy** (`viewWorld.ts`): not the `World` but a `ViewWorld`. Every frame carries the tick, the nodes, the bots without their memory (`ViewBot`: `memoryCount` and `learnedCount` instead) and the small totals (events, births, deaths by cause, eaten, command count). The big lists (samples, log, commands, the selected bot with its full memory) are slow data: sent at most about four times a second, when paused or stepping, and with every answer to a command, a selection, a reset or a load. The lineage is only sent on request, and (M15) one layer of the heat grid only while a heatmap is on (item 7). Panels take a `ViewWorld`; `checkCommand` (the hover ghost and the greyed event cards) runs on the page against it. The bots of a frame are packed into two typed arrays (`PackedBots`, `botColumns.ts`: one row per bot, 12 Float64 and 9 Int32 columns, 132 bytes a bot) whose buffers are transferred, not cloned; `applyFrame` builds the same `ViewBot` objects from them on the page, so no panel changed. At 2,000 bots a frame's bots went from about 780 KB and 4.3 ms to build and clone (Node) to 264 KB and 0.23 ms, and unpacking them on the page costs 0.09 ms (Node) or 0.25 ms (Chrome).
   - **Store API** (`store.ts`): `dispatch` returns a `Promise<CommandResult>`, `exportSave` a `Promise<Save>`, `advance` and `stepOnce` a promise that resolves when their ticks are shown, `startLoad` and `cancelLoad` go through messages (a cancel resolves the load as cancelled when the worker confirms), `settled()` resolves when every earlier message has been answered (tests use it). `resetWorld` still returns a boolean at once, and the new world appears with the worker's first frame. `queryFamily` and `queryFounders` wrap the queries. All the load rules in item 4 hold unchanged.
6. `[PROVISIONAL]` The family panel (M14, built) lets the player study lineages. It reads the lineage records through the worker's `query` message (`queryFamily`, `queryFounders` in `store.ts`) and changes no sim rule, number or hash.
   - **What it shows**: for the selected bot (or any record picked in the panel) the record's status, three counters (living descendants, total descendants, deepest generation), the mother's line as a chain (nearest first, 5 by default, "+n older" asks for up to 40, "Earlier ancestors not kept" when the lineage was pruned; each chip also has a "father #n" button that re-centres the tree on that father; the father of the shown record is in its record details) and the descendants as an open-and-close tree (3 levels open by default, at most 12 children per node and 200 rows, then "+n more"). With nothing selected it shows the founder lines (founders, bots you placed, and in a long run the oldest line whose ancestors were pruned) with living and total descendants, most living first.
   - **How it behaves**: a living bot's row selects it on the map and in the inspector; a dead bot's row (or an ancestor chip, or a founder line) points the panel at that record, which shows its born and died ticks, cause, sex, mother, father and birth traits (a parent whose record is pruned reads "record not kept"). A bot belongs to the lines of both parents. The map's dotted family lines for the selected bot join its living mother, father and children; the population traits panel starts with a Females / Males count of the bots alive. "Focus" re-centres the tree on any row. The inspector has "Show family tree" for the selected bot (and for a dead one). Status is always text first (alive, or "died at t 12,345 · Starved") with a glyph as a second cue. The tree is plain HTML for keyboard and screen reader use: arrow keys move, right opens or goes to the first child, left closes or goes to the parent, Home and End jump, one row at a time is in the tab order. On a narrow panel the indentation stops after 4 levels and deeper rows show "+n deeper".
   - **Data flow**: the panel asks again when the focus changes and about once a second while the run is going (a paused run only if the tick moved), keeps the last answer on screen while the next one is on its way, and ignores answers that arrive after a newer request. The tree box has a fixed height and scrolls, so a refresh moves nothing. 7. `[PROVISIONAL]` Heatmap overlays (M15, built) show where things happen: bot traffic, births and deaths, so cause and effect becomes spatial ("where do bots die around this danger node?"). It changes no sim rule, number, RNG draw or hash, and `src/sim` and `src/content` are untouched.
   - **Where the heat lives**: outside the `World`. `heat.ts` (pure, DOM-free, in `src/ui`) holds a `HeatGrid` of the map's size (120 x 80 in Eden): per layer (`traffic`, `births`, `deaths`) an all-time count (`Float64Array`) and a `recent` count (`Float32Array`). `observeWorld(heat, world)` is called by whatever runs `step`: the `SimHost` after every step and after every applied command (a meteor kills at command time), and the replay job through an `onTick(world)` hook (`createReplayJob`'s last parameter) after every step and every applied command. The grid is rebuilt exactly by a load because the replay observes the same moments; it is not hashed and not saved (`src/persist` is unchanged; a save is still seed + commands). It is always accumulated, whether or not a layer is shown, so switching one on shows the whole history. A load builds its own grid and replaces the live one only when it completes; a failed or cancelled load keeps the old grid with the old world.
   - **What is counted**: traffic is +1 per living bot per tick in the cell of `floor(x), floor(y)`. Births and deaths come from the change in `totalBirths` and in the death total since the last look; that many of the newest `birth` or `death` entries in `World.log` (both carry the child's or the dead bot's x and y) go in their cells. The log is a ring of 500 events, so if events ever fell out before they were seen (not possible while the host observes after every step), they are counted in `HeatGrid.unattributed` instead of being put in the wrong cell, and an entry older than the last look is never counted twice.
   - **Recent**: the `recent` grids are multiplied by 0.9 every 100 ticks (a half-life of about 658 ticks) and a value under 0.001 is set to 0. The constants (`HEAT_DECAY`, `HEAT_DECAY_EVERY`, `HEAT_BIN_COUNT`) are in `heat.ts`, not `tuning.ts`, since none of them changes behaviour.
   - **Protocol (decision)**: the reserved `heat` request is now `heat{layer: 'traffic'|'births'|'deaths'|null, range: 'all'|'recent'}`, and the answer is its own message, `heatSnapshot{heat}`, not a field of the frame. A separate message keeps the frame channel free when the heatmap is off, and the snapshot has its own cadence. The snapshot is one layer as a `Float32Array` of 6,144 cells plus the summary (`max`, `hottest` cell, `total`, `tick`, `runId`); structured clone copies it (about 25 KB, about 20 to 25 microseconds to build). It is sent only while a layer is on: at once after a `heat` request, after every applied command, a reset and a completed load, with every advance that asks for slow data (paused or stepping), and otherwise at most about four times a second (`HEAT_INTERVAL_MS`, 250). It rides after the frame it belongs to. The page keeps the latest snapshot that matches its layer, range and run (`WorldSnapshot.heat`), and ignores any other.
   - **Cost** (measured with `observeWorld` timed beside `step`, in Node): about 6 microseconds a tick at about 64 bots (2% of a step) and about 80 microseconds a tick at about 1,270 bots (1.7% of a step); the fade pass over the three recent grids runs once every 100 ticks.
   - **Drawing**: `heatRender.ts` turns the snapshot into an offscreen canvas of the map's size (120 x 80 in Eden) (one pixel per cell, rebuilt once per snapshot) and `render.ts` stretches it over the map right after the grid and before the zones, nodes and bots, smoothing off so cells stay square. Each layer has its own hue (M19; before that one neutral warm-white ramp served all three) in the same five steps of growing opacity (0.20, 0.34, 0.48, 0.62, 0.78), an empty cell clear: amber (255, 168, 40) for traffic, pink (255, 128, 190) for births, orange (255, 92, 56) for deaths. They were picked to stay readable over the dark map and off the node colours (green resource, red danger, blue water, purple ruin, cyan shelter) and the gold bots; the swatches of the legend and a small marker on the "Under cursor" readout use the layer's hue, while the layer name stays in the radio group and the legend text, so colour is never the only cue. Traffic is on a square-root scale (step k ends at k squared over 25 of the maximum), births and deaths are linear (k over 5). The cell under the pointer gets a thin white outline.
   - **Controls and legend** (`HeatLegend.tsx`, a panel in its own grid area `heat` right under the map's status line, so nothing above or below the map in flow changes height and `--map-chrome` still holds): a layer choice None / Traffic / Births / Deaths and a range choice All time / Recent, both radio groups (one tab stop, arrow keys, Home and End, 44 px buttons; the range is disabled with a reason while no layer is on); key H cycles None, Traffic, Births, Deaths (ignored while typing in a field, and with Ctrl, Alt, Meta or Shift; no clash with 1-7, Esc, D, B, P, M); a numeric key to the five steps (for example `1-30`, `31-121`: whole numbers for all time, decimals for recent, "none" for a step no whole number can fall in); the peak value with its cell coordinates and the total; and a reserved-height line "Under cursor (34, 12): 17 deaths" driven by `hoverCell` in the store (set by the map's pointer or keyboard cursor; a separate small store so moving the pointer re-renders only the legend). The numbers carry the meaning, so the map never relies on colour alone. The choice lives in the page's store only (not saved, forgotten on reload).
   - **Verified numbers** (seed a, eden, ticks 4,998 and 60,000): the totals and peaks shown in Chrome (traffic peak 1,648 at (12, 43) next to the west spring; births peaks at shelters, deaths at the north-west grove) equal a Node replay of the same run, and after save, restart and load the six layer and range summaries are identical.

10. `[PROVISIONAL]` **Predators** (M21a mechanics, SIM_VERSION 6; M21b put three in Eden, gave the UI an Add predator tool and retuned `digestTicks` to 1300, SIM_VERSION 7). The user approved the fifth RNG stream `predator` (section 8 item 2). Retune and the user-facing parts are M21b and M21c; every number below is a first guess in `tuning.predator` and `nodeDefs.predator`.
   - **Node**: kind `predator`, `radius` 4 (the threat radius bots remember and avoid) and `damage` 35 (a bite per tick), plus `WorldNode.hunt` (null for every other kind; hashed, after `expires`): position at the start of its turn (`px`, `py`, for drawing), `heading`, home spot, `targetId`, `chase`, `restUntil` and `kills`. At most `predator.maxCount` (8) exist: `placeNode` is refused beyond it ("Too many predators (8)."). Predators do not count toward `player.maxNodes`; they follow the usual 1-cell gap rule between predators; `removeNode` works as for any node. Meteors, plague and craters do not affect them.
   - **Movement** (`predators.ts`, `movePredators`, once per tick right after the tick counter goes up and before any bot acts; id order): each predator takes one `predator` draw per tick (a wander turn of up to `bot.turnRate`), whatever it does, plus two per territory try when a cub is attempted (Ecology bullet below; none with ecology off); a world with no predator takes no draws. While `tick <= restUntil` it stands still. Otherwise it targets the nearest living bot (ties: lowest id) that is outside every shelter's radius and within `senseRadius` (8) of its centre, and walks at `speed` (0.9) straight toward it; each tick of chasing adds one to `chase`, and at `chaseTicks` (40) it gives up: rest `giveUpRest` (60) ticks, target cleared. With no target it wanders, steered back toward home when more than `homeRange` (11) away. It never enters a shelter's radius (it tries headings turned by `steerStep` until one is outside, else stands still; one placed inside a shelter may only walk outward) and the map edge turns it around.
   - **Bites and kills**: in `exposureAt` (nodes.ts), a bot outside every shelter within `biteRange` (1) of the centre of a predator that is not resting takes its damage into `Exposure.predator` (several predators add up; the credited one is the lowest id). A bot that dies of cause `predator` makes that predator count a kill and rest `digestTicks` (300), so one predator kills at most one bot per rest. The dead bot leaves a normal ruin. Shelters are therefore a refuge from predators as well as the place to breed.
   - **Memory**: a bot senses a predator by its centre within its sense radius (every other kind: by its edge). It remembers the sighting position in a third slot class, `memory.predatorCap` (2) on top of the usual caps, and forgets it after `memoryTicks` (400) without seeing it again. Sightings count as dangers for fleeing, steering, and the food and water choices (a bot does not walk to food inside the threat zone), and a desperate bot ignores them as it ignores danger. Family sharing passes them (`family.shareKinds` includes `predator`), and stale ones are dropped by the same age rule.
   - **Ecology** (E1 mechanics, E2 retune and switch-on, `[PROVISIONAL]`: `predator.ecology.enabled` is true in Eden, `SIM_VERSION` 10, save format unchanged; saves from version 9 replay to a different hash. Controls that pin pre-ecology numbers run on `staticPredators`, and `--static-predators` gives the old fixed predators). With it on, a world created with it gets `World.ecology` (dens and counters) and every predator `hunt.life` (energy, age, origin, parent, kills since its last cub); with it off those keys are absent, not null. Numbers (`tuning.predator.ecology`): `drain` 0.02 energy a tick, `killEnergy` 60, `maxEnergy` 100, `huntBelow` 60 (a predator at or above it does not target or bite, it wanders home, and still takes its one draw), `maxAge` 12000, `adultAge` 1500, `killsPerCub` 6, `cubEnergy` 50, `cubCost` 30, `homeTries` 8, `homeSpacing` 24, `homeMargin` 4, `denRadius` 4, `recolonizeTicks` 3000. `predatorLife` runs once a tick after the world events expire and before family sharing: ageing, starvation at 0 energy and death at `maxAge` (node removed, `predator` log event `starved` or `aged`, bots forget it like any removed node); then cubs, in id order: a predator with `killsPerCub` kills since its last cub, age at least `adultAge`, energy of at least `cubCost` + `huntBelow` (a kill tops energy up to `maxEnergy` and the drain comes off before this check, so a cub needs `cubCost` + `huntBelow` <= `maxEnergy` - `drain`; `checkEcology` refuses numbers that break it, and `cubEnergy` must stay below `huntBelow`) and the world below `predator.maxCount` tries up to `homeTries` spots (two `predator` draws each: an angle and a distance of `homeSpacing` to twice that around its own home), valid when `homeMargin` inside the map, outside every shelter and at least `homeSpacing` from every other home; the first valid spot wins, a cub appears at the parent with that home and `cubEnergy`, and the parent pays `cubCost` and restarts its kill count (no valid spot: nothing changes); then dens in array order: each starting or placed predator makes a den (cubs never do), a den with a predator homed within `denRadius` is occupied, an empty one is timed and after `recolonizeTicks` a newcomer arrives at the nearest map-edge point (ties left, right, top, bottom; no draw; `predator.maxCount` still holds) homed at the den. A `removeNode` of a predator removes its den unless another predator is homed there. Samples gain `predators`. The survey gains the columns `preds pMin pMax pBorn pArr pStarve pAged huntGap idle` `pZero` and `pCap%` (E2: the longest predator-free run and the share of ticks at `predator.maxCount`) and `--static-predators`. **Prey rule** (P1 mechanics, P2 retune and switch-on, `[PROVISIONAL]`, `SIM_VERSION` 12, save format unchanged; saves from version 11 replay to a different hash): `tuning.predator.prey` = `{ enabled: true, perPredator: 16, minCount: 1, leaveAfter: 500 }`, and `ecology.killsPerCub` went from 6 to 3 (the limit now holds the count down, so a cub can come sooner). The limit is `min(maxCount, max(minCount, floor(bots / perPredator)))`; `predatorLife` order: age and starve, leaving, cubs, dens; cubs and newcomers are gated by the limit instead of `maxCount` (a gated parent takes no draws); over the limit for `leaveAfter` ticks the lowest-energy predator that was not player-placed (ties lowest id) leaves (`left` log event, no draw); `placeNode` keeps the hard `maxCount` cap. `World.ecology.prey = {overSince, left}`. **Heritable traits** (H1 mechanics, H2 retune and switch-on, `[PROVISIONAL]`, `SIM_VERSION` 13, save format unchanged; saves from version 12 replay to a different hash): `tuning.predator.traits` = `{ enabled: true, mutation: { rate: 0.5, strength: 0.2 }, bounds: { sense 0.75 to 1.5, speed 0.8 to 1.2, stamina 0.5 to 2 }, price: { sense 0.15, speed 0.3, stamina 0.06 } }` (only the mutation strength and the three prices moved from the H1 guesses 0.1 and 0.05 / 0.1 / 0.02; bounds untouched). `src/sim/predatorTraits.ts`; six extra `predator` draws per cub (taken once a territory is found); drain is `ecology.drain x (1 + sum of price x (trait - 1))`; range is `senseRadius x sense`, step `speed x speed trait`, give-up after `chaseTicks x stamina`. The pre-H2 world is `--set predator.traits.enabled=false`; survey columns `pSense pSpeed pStam pGen`. Harness: `--no-prey`, survey columns `pCorr pCorrL pLag pLeft` (correlation of the predator count with the bot count, taken every `stats.sampleEvery` ticks, at lag 0 and at the best lag).

## 10. Open Questions

- Two sexes numbers (M17): the retune (eatRate 10 instead of 5, reproCooldown 100, birthCost 10, founders' reproThreshold 65) is the price of two sexes plus a mate search; with it the eden seeds survive but small colonies after a drought or plague die out or survive by luck of the seed (several nearby settings failed an event test on one seed). Should males and females both search, or should the sense radius for mates be larger than for food? Should the female's shelter requirement stay?

- Large populations: with typed-array frames the frame transfer no longer matters at 2,000 bots, but the map draw does (about 1,000 bots: 70 to 90 fps in the browser pane, 1,450 bots: about 30 to 55 fps; same before and after). Separately, every command answers with a frame that carries the slow data, so placing about 1,450 bots one command at a time took 40 s (the same before and after the change). Neither matters at the eden preset's 25 to 80 bots; batching commands or skipping the slow data for repeated commands is the lever if it ever does.
- Heatmaps (M15): (resolved in M19: each layer has its own hue.) Is "recent" (half-life about 658 ticks) the right window, and should the player be able to change it? At 1280x800 the heatmap controls sit just under the map, so the layer buttons are visible but the legend is a short scroll away (key H works anywhere); is that acceptable or should the legend float over the map? The grid is never saved, so loading a 60,000-tick run (about 25 s) is also what rebuilds it.
- Cross-engine hashes (resolved 2026-11-11): a run now gives the same state hash in Node and in Chrome at 1,000, 5,000, 20,000 and 60,000 ticks (seeds a, b, j, zz-7 checked). The cause was `Math.cos`, `sin`, `atan2` and `log`, whose last bit differs between the V8 builds of Node and Chrome (first divergence: tick 1, bot 101's x, 3.036558786856637 vs 3.0365587868566366). `src/sim/exactMath.ts` now supplies bit-exact `sin`, `cos`, `atan`, `atan2`, `log`, `exp`, `pow` and `hypot` built only from + - * / and sqrt, and `tests/architecture.test.ts` bans the inexact `Math` functions and `**` in `src/sim`. Saves made before this fix hash differently and load with the "different version" warning. Firefox and Safari were not tested (the rule makes them agree in principle).
- Lineage (M11): are 3,000 records (pruned to 2,400) the right cap? A 60,000-tick eden run makes 1,900 to 4,100 births, so about half the seeds prune; a pruned tree loses its extinct branches and its oldest ancestors, so the founders' lines fade after a long run. Hashing the state is about 45 ms with the store full; since M13 it happens in the worker, so autosave no longer stalls the page. The family panel (M14) reads these records through the worker's `query` message; the founder-lines table is where a pruned run shows up (lines starting at a high generation).
- Family panel (M14): is the tree too busy on a long run (a line can have hundreds of descendants; only 12 children per node and 200 rows are listed, ranked by living descendants)? Would a search by bot id, or a view of the whole forest, help? "+n older" is an upper bound (it counts generations, and pruned records make the real chain shorter). Below about 300 px wide the tree box scrolls sideways a little.
- Family sharing (M10): is the effect what the user wants? It removes about 60% of thirst deaths (knowledge of springs travels down a family line) and shifts mean evolved caution down a little, but it is mostly invisible on the map. Is the dotted family line plus the inspector rows enough to see it, or is a lineage view (the planned M11) or a stat such as "share of memory learned from family" needed? Are the numbers right (`shareRadius` 3, `shareEvery` 10)? If a run ever became too stable, the retune order is: raise `shareEvery`, shrink `shareRadius`, drop `resource` from `shareKinds`, inherit only. Saves made before M10 load with the different-version warning (the state gained `learnedFrom` and `familyLearned`); saves now carry a simulation version (see the Saves question).
- Saves (M9): share links are compressed (see section 8, item 4), so even a 5,000-command run fits; a very noisy run (hand-made saves with full-precision coordinates) can still be too long and goes to a file. Should a save also keep the pause state, speed or selection, and should a shared link carry a note or title? A save made before a rule or tuning change loads with a hash warning and may play out differently. Resolved 2026-11-13: a save carries `sim` and the warning says whether the save is older or newer than the game (rules v1 today).

- Which extra node purposes are needed beyond water (for example disease, trade)?
- Are drought, bounty, plague and meteor (M8) the right set, and are the sizes right? A drought halves the population on average and the lowest value across seeds was 10 of about 42; an aimed plague costs about a quarter; a meteor costs about 3 bots and is mostly a local, visible strike (a bigger radius or damage would make it matter more). Should bots perceive and avoid a plague (see section 7 item 3)? Should events stack or exclude each other (a drought and a bounty together multiply today)? Further events (disease spread, migration, a flood) are not designed.
- Is the M5 observer UI enough for cause and effect? Old player commands no longer scroll out of the event log (it lists them from `World.commands`); births, deaths and world-event ends are still limited to the newest 500, so an old death can leave the log. Is that enough?
- Is there any player goal beyond observation and intervention?
- Numbers: the eden preset (40 bots, 34 food nodes plus 3 groves, 4 danger, 12 shelter, 8 springs) with ruins and thirst holds 24 to 74 bots across seeds a..j after warmup over 20,000 ticks (21 to 82 over 60,000, never extinct). That is the post-retune figure; the numbers below it in this paragraph are from before the retune. Ruins supply about 5 to 9% of the food eaten over 20,000 ticks and 7 to 10% over 60,000. Thirst deaths are 10 to 29% of all deaths per seed over 20,000 ticks and 8 to 22% over 60,000 (starvation is still 55 to 80%), and danger deaths stay rare (0 to 11 per 20,000 ticks, 0 to 58 per 60,000). Water cut the population against the pre-water numbers (27 to 76 and 27 to 89), mostly at the top end. Is that the feel the user wants (swings are about 2x, and the founders still all die of age together near tick 3000)? The old open point that ruins make a danger zone tempting for a desperate bot was handled in M12 (ruins in danger decay 5x faster, and caution sets how early a bot is desperate); see the M12 changelog entry for the numbers.
- Traits (M6, re-checked after M7b): over 60,000 ticks the reproduction threshold falls from 80 to about 57 to 63 on every seed (breeding early pays), metabolism stays near its founder value (means 0.79 to 1.03), sense radius wanders (4.3 to 6.8), and caution varies a lot between seeds (0.26 to 0.82, measured before M12; since M12 the 60,000-tick means are 0.46 to 0.86 on every seed, mean 0.60, because caution now has a cost: item 3 of section 5). Water made a plain `metabolism x thirst` cost too weak (a fast bot found springs sooner than it burned energy), and a cost that also rewarded slow bots pinned them to the lower bound, hence `max(1, metabolism)` cubed (item 9 of section 5). Are those the trait stories the user wants to watch, or should the costs be tuned to keep more variety? Sense radius is not rewarded more strongly by water: a radius of 12 still loses to 6 in the selection test.
- Inbreeding numbers (M18): are lifespanLoss 0.2 / energyLoss 0 / healthLoss 0.2 the right size? About 19% of eden births are inbred at these settings (mates are found by sensing within a few cells, so relatives meet often). Should there be a mate-choice bias against relatives, or is the penalty alone enough?
- Aging fertility numbers (M16): are 200 / 600 / 1,800 / 2,600 and a 50% starting fertility the right shape? They were not retuned (the defaults passed `tests/tuning.test.ts` and `tests/events.test.ts` first time). Births fell 9% on eden, and mean parent age at a birth fell from 1,031 to 956 ticks instead of rising, because breeding stops 400 ticks before the end of life and a young bot's longer cooldown is a small effect next to that.
